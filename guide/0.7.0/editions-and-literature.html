IX Editions and literature

A publication, the edition a text was transcribed from or the literature on it, is a biblStruct: in the source description of a file that transcribes an edition (II.3 Publication and source); in the standOff's listBibl, as a record that witnesses and other records point to (V.7 Witnesses); in a manuscript's literature (VIII.9 Images, literature, sources). The application keeps publications as records modelled on biblatex, related to one another, a text in a volume and a volume in a series, and one kind serves for the editions and the literature. Pages belong to the relation, not to the publication: a council's canons are in Werminghoff's volume at pages 318 to 421, and the volume's record says nothing of them.

The source description's biblStruct is common, since most files transcribe an edition; its entry type and a biblatex key are full.

IX.1 A publication's parts

A biblStruct has up to three parts, each with its title, whose level says which part it belongs to:

  • analytic: a text with its own title in a larger publication, a council in a volume of councils, an article in a journal; its title is level a, and it names its own author or editor where it has one;
  • monogr: the publication itself, a book or a volume (its title level m, a second title typed sub where the title page has one) or a journal (j); its authors or editors; its imprint, with the publisher, the place and the date; and, in biblScope, the volume, part or issue, and the pages where the text of the analytic stands;
  • series: the series the volume belongs to, its title level s, its editor where it has one, and the volume's number in it, in biblScope.

The edition of the Monumenta Germaniae Historica is the case the parts are made for: a series with its editor, the society that publishes it; a titled volume, edited by a scholar; and in it the texts. The Council of Aachen of 816:

<biblStruct>
 <analytic>
  <title level="a">Concilium Aquisgranense a. 816 (mensibus Augusto et Septembri)</title>
 </analytic>
 <monogr>

   <persName>
    <forename>Albert</forename>
    <surname>Werminghoff</surname>
   </persName>
  </editor>
  <title level="m" type="main">Concilia aevi Karolini</title>
  <title level="m" type="sub">Pars I</title>
  <imprint>
   <publisher>impensis Bibliopolii Hahniani</publisher>
   <pubPlace>Hannover</pubPlace>
   <pubPlace>Leipzig</pubPlace>
   <date>1906</date>
  </imprint>
  <biblScope unit="part">1</biblScope>
  <biblScope unit="page">307-464</biblScope>
 </monogr>
 <series>
  <editor>
   <orgName>Societas Aperiendis Fontibus Rerum Germanicarum Medii Aevi</orgName>
  </editor>
  <title level="s">Monumenta Germaniae Historica</title>
  <title level="s">Concilia</title>
  <biblScope unit="volume">2.1</biblScope>
 </series>
 <citedRange unit="page">318-421</citedRange>
</biblStruct>

IX.2 Pages

A publication has two kinds of pages, and each has its element:

  • biblScope in monogr, typed by its unit page, says where the text of the analytic stands in the volume: Aachen's council at pages 307 to 464 of the MGH's volume. It's part of the publication's description, and the import relates the text to the volume by it;
  • citedRange, after the monogr and the series, says which pages the file's text comes from: the canons at pages 318 to 421. They're the witness's pages, the relation's. Where the file transcribes the whole text of the analytic, there's none: its pages are the witness's.

Either may give its range in from and to beside its words. An older edition printed in columns counts columns: the letter of Pope Celestine I to the bishops of Vienne and Narbonne, in Coustant's edition of the popes' letters:

<biblStruct>
 <analytic>
  <title level="a">Epistola IV / Caelestini papae I. ad episcopos prouinciae Viennensis &amp;
     Narbonensis</title>
 </analytic>
 <monogr>
  <editor>
   <persName>
    <forename>Pierre</forename>
    <surname>Coustant</surname>
   </persName>
  </editor>
  <title level="m" type="main">Epistolae Romanorum pontificum, et quae ad eos scriptae sunt a S. Clemente I.
     usque ad Innocentium III</title>
  <title level="m" type="sub">Tom. I: ab anno Christi 67 ad annum 440</title>
  <imprint>
   <publisher>Ludovicus-Dionysius Delatour</publisher>
   <publisher>Antonius-Urbanus Coustelier</publisher>
   <pubPlace>Paris</pubPlace>
   <date>1721</date>
  </imprint>
  <biblScope unit="volume">1</biblScope>
  <biblScope unit="column" from="1065"
   to="1072">1065-1072</biblScope>
 </monogr>
</biblStruct>

The units are a closed list, since each is a field of biblatex: volume, part, issue, page and column. A volume in a series is the volume's number in the series, a tome or a fascicle of it too; an issue is a journal's, a Heft. Another unit fails the RelaxNG:

Breaks scope-unit:

 <monogr>
  <title level="m">Concilia exempli gratia</title>
  <imprint>
   <date>1906</date>
  </imprint>
 </monogr>
 <series>
  <title level="s">Monumenta exempli gratia</title>
  <biblScope unit="tome">2</biblScope>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 </series>
</biblStruct>

Breaks cited-range-unit:

IX.3 Entry types and biblatex

The application's publications are biblatex's entries, and the import and the exports turn one into the other by the tables below. A publication's entry type is in type, by biblatex's name; where it's missing, the parts say which it is:

The entry type, where type doesn't give it
The partsbiblatex's type
an analytic, in a monogr whose title is level jarticle
an analytic, in a monogr whose title is level mincollection
a monogr alone, with editors and no authorcollection
a monogr alone, otherwisebook

A thesis, a report or a page on the web looks like a book in its parts, and so says what it is by type; an export always does, so that a publication exported and imported again keeps its type. The types are biblatex's, a vocabulary whose seeds are biblatex's own types; another is reported, to be checked or added. A biblatex key, where a publication has one, is an idno typed biblatex in the part the entry describes. A made-up thesis:

<listBibl>
 <biblStruct type="thesis"
  xml:id="new_bibl_exemplum_thesis">
  <monogr>
   <author>
    <persName>
     <forename>Anna</forename>
     <surname>Exempla</surname>
    </persName>
   </author>
   <title level="m">Canones exempli gratia</title>
   <idno type="biblatex">exempla2020</idno>
   <imprint>
    <publisher>Universitas Exemplensis</publisher>
    <date>2020</date>
   </imprint>
  </monogr>
 </biblStruct>
</listBibl>

Breaks entry-type:

IX.4 Publications as records

A publication that more than one thing refers to, the edition several witnesses are in, the catalogue a manuscript's literature cites, is listed once in the standOff's listBibl, with an xml:id, a record's or a placeholder's (new_bibl_ and a name), and pointed to: a witness by a ptr and its pages in citedRange (V.7 Witnesses), a manuscript's literature the same way. The import makes the application's records from a biblStruct: the volume, the text in it and the series, related to one another; then the file's witness, in the publication at the pages of the citedRange. An export writes each publication it points to as a biblStruct with its entry type.

IX.5 Reconstructions

A reconstruction is a text put together from others: a collection known only from what later collections took from it, or a text the project assembles from several editions. Its file's type is reconstruction (II.1.1 Categories and types), and its source description names the publication it comes from, says in a note that it's a reconstruction, and lists what it was made from in a listWit: the editions, for one the project put together; the manuscripts, for one a scholar made from them. The collection of Benedictus Levita, which the project puts together from Pertz's edition and Schmitz's of the first Additio:

<sourceDesc>
 <biblStruct xml:id="pertz-1837">
  <analytic>
   <title level="a">Benedicti Capitularia</title>
  </analytic>
  <monogr>
   <editor>
    <persName>
     <forename>Georg Heinrich</forename>
     <surname>Pertz</surname>
    </persName>
   </editor>
   <title level="m">Supplementa tomi I</title>
   <imprint>
    <publisher>Hahnsche Buchhandlung</publisher>
    <pubPlace>Hannover</pubPlace>
    <date>1837</date>
   </imprint>
   <biblScope unit="page">17-158</biblScope>
  </monogr>
  <series>
   <title level="s">Monumenta Germaniae Historica</title>
   <title level="s">Leges</title>
   <biblScope unit="volume">2.2</biblScope>
  </series>
  <note>A reconstruction: the collection as the project puts it together, from Pertz's edition for the whole
     text, and from Schmitz's for Additio 1, which Pertz's lacks.</note>
 </biblStruct>
 <biblStruct xml:id="schmitz-2008">
  <monogr>
   <editor>
    <persName>
     <forename>Gerhard</forename>
     <surname>Schmitz</surname>
    </persName>
   </editor>
   <title level="m">Additio 1</title>
   <idno type="url">https://www.benedictus.mgh.de/edition/aktuell/additio1.pdf</idno>
   <imprint>
    <publisher>Gerhard Schmitz</publisher>
    <date>2008</date>
   </imprint>
  </monogr>
 </biblStruct>
 <listWit>
  <witness xml:id="wit-pertz">
   <bibl>
    <ptr target="#pertz-1837"/>
    <citedRange unit="page" from="17"
     to="158">17–158</citedRange>
   </bibl>: the whole text but Additio 1</witness>
  <witness xml:id="wit-schmitz">
   <bibl>
    <ptr target="#schmitz-2008"/>
   </bibl>: Additio 1</witness>
 </listWit>
</sourceDesc>

The import makes the reconstruction a version whose witness is the publication, and joins it to the witnesses it lists. The template reconstruction.xml holds the whole header.
