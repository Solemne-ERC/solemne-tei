The SOLEMNE Encoding Guidelines

VII Dates

Everything dated has one kind of date: a council and a person's life (V Records), a manuscript's unit, its hands and its binding (VIII Manuscripts), a relation, such as an owner's from 1460 to 1520 (VI Relations), and a date the text names (IV.6 Dates). A date keeps the words its source gives ("sec. IX ultimo terzo", "ERA DLXXXVII"), and what they mean: a point or a span, its bounds, its precision (a century, a year, a month, a day), whether it's circa or uncertain, and the calendar or era of its words. The application searches every date by its bounds, shows it in its words, and exchanges it in EDTF, the Extended Date/Time Format of ISO 8601-2.

Nothing in this part is required. date is common, since the editions' imprints carry one; the rest is full.

VII.1 The one form

A date is written once, in one form, whatever element carries it:

  • its words, as the element's text, where the element has text (date, origDate, birth, floruit); a record's date may have none;
  • its bounds, always where they can be given, in TEI's attributes, as years of four digits (0813), months (0816-08) or days (0813-06-13):
    • a point known to its precision: when;
    • a point somewhere between two dates: notBefore and notAfter;
    • a span, such as a bishop's years or an owner's: from and to;
  • its EDTF, where the bounds can't say what the words do: in when-custom for a point, in from-custom and to-custom for a span's ends, with datingMethod pointing to the file's declaration of EDTF, #edtf (VII.2 Calendars and eras). EDTF says:
    • circa, by ~ (0800~), uncertain, by ? (0756?), or both, by %;
    • a century as the interval of its years (0801/0900), and a part of one as its own (0867/0900);
    • a decade by unspecified digits (081X);
    • a point among a few dates, by a set ([0755,0756]).

The import makes one date of them: the words, the bounds, the precision (from the EDTF, or from the bounds' form), circa and certainty (from the EDTF's marks), and the calendar. An export writes the same form. The application's date form proposes the bounds and the EDTF from the words; an encoder may write them by hand. TEI's other forms of the same date, its ISO attributes (when-iso, …), a named period (period), and the custom bounds (notBefore-custom, notAfter-custom), aren't taken: one form.

A century is the commonest date of a manuscript, and counts as catalogues mean it: "sec. X" is 901 to 1000, in the bounds and in the EDTF, which is the interval of its years, 0901/1000. Not 09XX, which EDTF reads as 900 to 999, a year off at each end (VII.3 What the checks require):

<origDate notBefore="0901" notAfter="1000"
 when-custom="0901/1000" datingMethod="#edtf"
 calendar="#gregorian">sec.
X</origDate>

A part of a century needs no EDTF: the bounds say it, and the words keep how it was said. Where one is given, it is its interval too (0867/0900 for the last third of the 9th century).

<origDate notBefore="0867" notAfter="0900"
 calendar="#gregorian">sec. IX ultimo terzo</origDate>

A circa date gives in its bounds how far the encoder, or the source, reads the circa; its EDTF keeps the year named:

<origDate notBefore="0776" notAfter="0825"
 when-custom="0800~" datingMethod="#edtf"
 calendar="#gregorian">sec.
VIII-IX (ca. 800)</origDate>

An uncertain year, and the years it may be instead, is a point between bounds with its EDTF marked uncertain. The edition of the Council of Aschheim dates it "a. 756 vel 755-60"; as a record of the council:

<event xml:id="new_event_aschheim"
 type="council" notBefore="0755" notAfter="0760"
 when-custom="0756?" datingMethod="#edtf">
 <eventName>Concilium Ascheimense</eventName>
</event>

A span has its two ends. In the records of the Council of Carthage of 397, Aurelius of Carthage flourishes from 388 to 423:

<person xml:id="new_person_aurelius_carthage">
 <persName type="main">Aurelius, archbishop of Carthage</persName>
 <floruit from="0388" to="0423">from 0388 to 0423</floruit>
</person>

Where a span's ends are uncertain, each end has its EDTF, and the bounds hold the earliest start and the latest end:

<person xml:id="new_person_exemplus_episcopus">
 <persName type="main">Exemplus, bishop</persName>
 <floruit from="0745" to="0760"
  from-custom="0750~" to-custom="0760" datingMethod="#edtf">c. 750 to
   760</floruit>
</person>

VII.2 Calendars and eras

The bounds are always in the Christian era, in the Gregorian calendar, as TEI defines them; EDTF is too. The words may reckon otherwise: by the Spanish era, 38 years ahead of the Christian, by the Julian calendar's days, by a king's years. calendar names the reckoning of the words, pointing to a calendar that the file declares once, in the profile description's calendarDesc; the bounds give what the words come to. Each declaration says what the calendar is, and may link to a description of it in target.

A manuscript's date (origDate) and a date in the text always name the calendar of their words, as the Bodleian's catalogues do for a manuscript's: #gregorian for a modern statement, such as a catalogue's "sec. IX" or "a. 1009", and the source's own otherwise, such as #julian for a day it gives in the Julian calendar, or an era. These are the dates whose calendar matters. A unit said undated names none. The dates of publications, of records and of the header are modern years in the Christian era, and name none either. The declarations the guide uses:

<calendarDesc>
 <calendar xml:id="gregorian">
  <p>The Gregorian calendar, in the Christian era: the calendar of modern statements of date.</p>
 </calendar>
 <calendar xml:id="edtf"
  target="https://www.loc.gov/standards/datetime/">
  <p>The Extended Date/Time Format (EDTF), ISO 8601-2: the form of the dates' custom attributes.</p>
 </calendar>
 <calendar xml:id="julian">
  <p>The Julian calendar, in which the sources give their days.</p>
 </calendar>
 <calendar xml:id="spanish-era">
  <p>The Spanish era, which counts from 38 BC.</p>
 </calendar>
</calendarDesc>

A day reckoned in the Julian calendar says so, and its bounds give the Gregorian day: later by 2 days in the 6th century, 3 in the 7th, 4 in the 8th and 9th, 5 in the 10th, 6 in the 11th, 7 in the 12th and 13th, each difference starting on 1 March of the century's first year. A month or a year keeps its units: "mense Augusto 822" is 0822-08, since a few days at its edges make no difference at its precision. The application shows a date in its words, and can give the Julian day back from the calendar. The Council of Valencia, 549, dates itself by the era, the king's years and the Roman day, which is 4 December in the Julian calendar:

<calendarDesc>
 <calendar xml:id="julian-valencia">
  <p>The Julian calendar.</p>
 </calendar>
 <calendar xml:id="spanish-era-valencia">
  <p>The Spanish era.</p>
 </calendar>
</calendarDesc>
<head type="inscription">ITEM <eventName ref="#new_event_valencia">VALLETANUM CONCILIUM</eventName>, VI
EPISCOPORUM HABITUM <date when="0549-12-06"
  calendar="#spanish-era-valencia #julian-valencia">ERA DLXXXVII
   ANNO XV <persName ref="#new_person_teudericus">TEUDERICI</persName> REGIS SUB DIE II NONAS
   DECEMBRES</date>
</head>

A manuscript's unit dated to the day, in a catalogue that gives the Julian day:

<calendarDesc>
 <calendar xml:id="julian-ans">
  <p>The Julian calendar.</p>
 </calendar>
</calendarDesc>
<origDate when="1009-03-29"
 calendar="#julian-ans">a. 1009, 23 marzo</origDate>

A manuscript's date or a date in the text without a calendar is an error, and so is a calendar the file doesn't declare:

Breaks date-calendar:

<origDate notBefore="0801" notAfter="0900">sec. IX</origDate>

Breaks calendar-declared:

<p>Habitum <date when="0549" calendar="#spanish-era">ERA DLXXXVII</date>.</p>

The ids of the declarations are the file's own; the examples above give each its own, since the guide's ids are unique, except gregorian and edtf, which the guide's examples take as declared. A file usually needs gregorian and edtf, and julian where it dates to the day.

VII.3 What the checks require

The RelaxNG checks each bound's form. The Schematron checks that a date reads one way:

  • a point between bounds gives both, and a span both its ends (warnings);
  • no bound comes after its pair: notBefore after notAfter, or from after to (an error);
  • an EDTF value has EDTF's form, as this part uses it, and its datingMethod (an error);
  • the bounds agree with the EDTF, by years: an exact date, an interval and a date with unspecified digits have the bounds' years exactly, so that 09XX goes with 900 to 999; a circa or uncertain one lies within the bounds (a warning);
  • a manuscript's date and a date in the text name their calendar (an error);
  • every calendar and datingMethod points to a declared calendar (an error).

Folios and pages also have from and to (locus, biblScope, citedRange): these rules leave them alone.

Breaks date-span-bounds:

<p>Datum <date notBefore="0755"
  calendar="#gregorian">post annum 755</date>.</p>

Breaks date-from-to:

<p>Episcopus <date from="0750" calendar="#gregorian">ab anno 750</date>.</p>

Breaks date-bounds-order:

<origDate notBefore="1000" notAfter="0901"
 calendar="#gregorian">sec. X</origDate>

Breaks date-edtf-form:

<origDate notBefore="0901" notAfter="1000"
 when-custom="sec. X" datingMethod="#edtf"
 calendar="#gregorian">sec.
X</origDate>

Breaks date-edtf-bounds:

<origDate notBefore="0901" notAfter="1000"
 when-custom="08XX" datingMethod="#edtf"
 calendar="#gregorian">sec.
X</origDate>

Breaks date-edtf-bounds:

<origDate notBefore="0901" notAfter="1000"
 when-custom="09XX" datingMethod="#edtf"
 calendar="#gregorian">sec.
X</origDate>
