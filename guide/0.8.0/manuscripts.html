The SOLEMNE Encoding Guidelines

VIII Manuscripts

A manuscript is described once, in an msDesc: in the source description of a file that transcribes a text from it (II The file and its header), or as a record in a listBibl (V Records). The description is TEI's own, restricted to what the application holds of a manuscript: the manuscript, its codicological units, each unit's contents and physical description, its hands and decoration, the binding, and the history of each. It is modelled on the Bodleian Libraries' guidelines for their catalogues, whose descriptions the project converts into its own (VIII.10 The Bodleian's descriptions).

The import makes the manuscript's record, its units, and an item for each text, joined to the work's record; it makes the relations a description states: where a unit was made, who owned the manuscript, which scribe a hand was. An export writes the same form. Everything in a description is full.

VIII.1 The description and the manuscript's identifier

A description holds the manuscript's identifier; its binding, in a physical description of its own; the history of the whole book, where its units were joined and who has owned it since; what is additional, its images, literature and sources; and its units, one at least, each with its contents, physical description and history. The Collectio 400 capitulorum's manuscript, Munich, Clm 4592, in short: the binding and the additional are left out, and of its three units, the one that holds the collection:

<msDesc>
 <msIdentifier>
  <settlement ref="#new_place_muenchen">München</settlement>
  <repository ref="#new_org_bsb">Bayerische Staatsbibliothek</repository>
  <idno type="shelfmark">Clm 4592</idno>
 </msIdentifier>
 <history>
  <provenance>
   <orgName ref="#new_org_benediktbeuern">SS. Benedikt und Jakob</orgName>,
     Benediktbeuern</provenance>
 </history>
 <msPart n="3">
  <msIdentifier>
   <idno>3, ff. 120–207</idno>
  </msIdentifier>
  <msContents>
   <msItem n="1"
    corresp="COLL.400.ms-Mun-BSB-4592">
    <locus from="158r" to="207v">ff. 158r–207v</locus>
    <title ref="#new_work_collectio_cd">Collectio CD capitulorum</title>
   </msItem>
  </msContents>
  <physDesc>
   <objectDesc form="codex">
    <supportDesc material="perg">
     <support>membr.</support>
     <extent>ff. 120–207, of ff. II, 207, II' in all <dimensions type="leaf" unit="mm">
       <height>240</height>
       <width>160</width>
      </dimensions>
     </extent>
    </supportDesc>
   </objectDesc>
  </physDesc>
  <history>
   <origin>
    <origPlace ref="#new_place_salzburg">Salzburg</origPlace>
    <origDate notBefore="0826"
     notAfter="0850" calendar="#gregorian">sec. IX secondo quarto</origDate>
   </origin>
  </history>
 </msPart>
</msDesc>

The file gives the physical description of the whole book too, and repeats it in each unit; the leaves are wrapped in num, the places and the library have no records, the units are numbered 11, 22 and 33, and the items point to the works by key. The template manuscript-collection.xml holds the whole description. Here, as everywhere in this part, the example is revised to the guide's forms.

The identifier names the manuscript's country, region and town, and its library, each pointing to its record by ref; the library's fonds, where it has one, in collection, the shelfmark still written whole in idno typed shelfmark; the manuscript's ids in other catalogues, each an idno typed by the authority (V.10 Ids elsewhere); its other identifiers, each an altIdentifier typed former (a former shelfmark), siglum (an editor's siglum) or code (the short code that files of the manuscript carry, Mun-BSB-4592); and its conventional names, in msName, one per language. A made-up manuscript:

<msIdentifier>
 <country ref="#new_place_exemplia">Exemplia</country>
 <region ref="#new_place_exemplia_borealis">Exemplia borealis</region>
 <settlement ref="#new_place_exemplum">Exemplum</settlement>
 <repository ref="#new_org_bibliotheca_exemplensis">Bibliotheca Exemplensis</repository>
 <collection ref="#new_org_codices_canonici">Codices canonici</collection>
 <idno type="shelfmark">Can. 12</idno>
 <idno type="mirabile">123456</idno>
 <altIdentifier type="former">
  <idno>Theol. 7</idno>
 </altIdentifier>
 <altIdentifier type="siglum">
  <idno>E</idno>
 </altIdentifier>
 <msName xml:lang="la">Codex Exemplensis</msName>
</msIdentifier>

A description holds only the elements this part names, each in its place: the contents and the physical description on a unit; on the manuscript, a physical description of the binding alone; no unit inside a unit; in adminInfo, the record's history alone; terms in a binding. Its prose, beside every value, holds a small set, so that what it mentions stays readable: locus; persName, placeName and orgName with ref; title with ref; date; foreign; hi, for italics and the like; and ref. A bibliography inside it keeps the forms of Part IX. Not the diplomatic set of the Bodleian's catalogues (<am>, <ex>, supplied, <damage>, <fw>, <figure>, <formula>, verse): quoting the manuscript is what transcriptions do. A count of leaves is words, without num. Each of these is an error; a description without a unit is one too:

Breaks manuscript-unit:

<msDesc>
 <msIdentifier>
  <settlement>Nowhere</settlement>
  <repository>National Library</repository>
  <idno type="shelfmark">MS 12</idno>
 </msIdentifier>
</msDesc>

Breaks description-elements:

<supportDesc material="perg">
 <support>Parchment.</support>
 <extent>ff. <num>207</num>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 </extent>
</supportDesc>

Breaks alt-identifier-type:

<msIdentifier>
 <settlement>Nowhere</settlement>
 <repository>National Library</repository>
 <idno type="shelfmark">MS 12</idno>
 <altIdentifier type="full_shelfmark">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
  <idno>Nowhere, National Library, MS 12</idno>
 </altIdentifier>
</msIdentifier>

VIII.2 Units

A unit (msPart) is a codicological unit: leaves made together, which the manuscript may have joined to others. Every description has one at least: a manuscript made in one go has one. A unit's identifier is an idno without a type, with its number or letter and its leaves (3, ff. 120–207); n gives its order. A unit holds its own contents, physical description and history; the manuscript holds only what belongs to the whole book: its binding, its history since its units were joined, and what is additional. A unit isn't divided into units: a unit made of smaller ones that were once a book of their own is described by its parts, each a unit of the manuscript, with that history in each.

Every unit is dated, in its origin's origDate (VIII.8 History), or said undated, by an origDate typed undated, holding the source's words if it has any, and no bounds. The import refuses neither, but reports a unit that says nothing:

<origDate type="undated">s. d.</origDate>

Breaks unit-dated:

<msPart>
 <msIdentifier>
  <idno>B</idno>
 </msIdentifier>
</msPart>
Element msPartchange

VIII.3 Contents

A unit's contents (msContents) are its items, each a text in it (msItem): its folios, by locus with from and to, or locusGrp for leaves apart; its author and its title, each pointing to its record by ref, the title to the work; the manuscript's own words, rubric, incipit, explicit, finalRubric and colophon; its languages, textLang; and notes. An item's parts are items inside it: a collection's register and prefaces, a text copied within another. Contents not listed one by one are said in summary. An item that a file transcribes names its transcription by corresp: the file's code, as source notes name documents (IV.4 Sources and the pointers' forms), or, in exports, the transcription's permalink; each file's description can point to every transcription of the manuscript. An item's category is its work's, not the item's. In Clm 4592, the Constitutum Silvestri is excerpted inside the collection:

<msContents>
 <msItem n="1"
  corresp="COLL.400.ms-Mun-BSB-4592">
  <locus from="158r" to="207v">ff. 158r–207v</locus>
  <title ref="#new_work_collectio_cd">Collectio CD capitulorum</title>
  <textLang mainLang="la">Latin</textLang>
  <msItem n="2">
   <locus from="194r" to="195r">ff. 194r–195r</locus>
   <author ref="#new_person_symmachus_pseudo">Symmachus papa pseudo</author>
   <title ref="#new_work_constitutum_silvestri">Constitutum Silvestri</title>
   <note>Excerpts, <foreign xml:lang="la">in collectionem CD capitulorum</foreign>.</note>
  </msItem>
 </msItem>
</msContents>

The file lists the excerpts as two more items of the unit, one with the author and one without, with escaped italics in their notes. An item without its folios is reported, since sources often give none:

Breaks item-locus:

<msContents>
 <msItem n="1">
  <title>Collectio CD capitulorum</title>
 </msItem>
</msContents>
Element msItemchange

VIII.4 The physical description

A unit's physical description (physDesc) may begin with paragraphs, then holds its parts, each in prose with its values in attributes: the object (objectDesc), its form in form (codex, roll, …); its support (supportDesc), the material in material (perg, chart, …), described in support, with the extent in words and the leaves' dimensions, their unit always given, foliation, collation and condition; its layout (layoutDesc), each layout with its columns, ruledLines and writtenLines; then its hands, decoration and additions. A unit's extent may say what the whole book counts. Forms and materials come from vocabularies that grow: a term outside the guide's seeds is reported, to be checked or added to the application's vocabulary (Appendix C The code tables and the vocabularies lists the seeds). Clm 4592's second unit:

<physDesc>
 <objectDesc form="codex">
  <supportDesc material="perg">
   <support>membr.</support>
   <extent>ff. 76–117 (78–119) <dimensions type="leaf" unit="mm">
     <height>240</height>
     <width>160</width>
    </dimensions>
   </extent>
  </supportDesc>
  <layoutDesc>
   <layout columns="2">2 colonne</layout>
  </layoutDesc>
 </objectDesc>
</physDesc>

The file gives the material only in words; the vocabulary's perg says it for the application. Dimensions without their unit are an error, and a form or a material outside the seeds is reported:

Breaks dimensions-unit:

<supportDesc material="perg">
 <support>Parchment.</support>
 <extent>ff. 207 <dimensions type="leaf">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
   <height>250</height>
   <width>170</width>
  </dimensions>
 </extent>
</supportDesc>

Breaks form-term:

<objectDesc form="libellus">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 <supportDesc material="perg">
  <support>Parchment.</support>
 </supportDesc>
</objectDesc>

Breaks material-term:

<supportDesc material="vellum">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 <support>Vellum.</support>
</supportDesc>

VIII.5 Hands

A unit's hands are its handDesc's handNotes, one per hand, each with an xml:id, its script and its execution from vocabularies (script, execution, which TEI lacks and the guide adds, as the Bodleian's guidelines do), its share of the unit (scope: sole, major or minor), its folios, and prose. The hands are counted from their notes. A scribe is named in the prose by persName with ref, and the hand is the scribe's by a relation (VI Relations), which can carry its certainty and evidence. A change in the text, an add, a del or a subst, points to the hand that made it by hand (III.9.6 Scribal changes and their hands); a hand pointed to in the file is described there. A made-up unit with two hands, a correction by the second, and the main hand's scribe:

<handDesc>
 <handNote xml:id="hand-A"
  script="minusculeCaroline" execution="libraria" scope="major">
  <locus from="1r" to="40v">ff. 1r–40v</locus>: the main hand, perhaps <persName ref="#new_person_exemplus_scriba">Exemplus</persName>, whose name stands at the foot of f. 40v.</handNote>
 <handNote xml:id="hand-B"
  script="minusculeCaroline" scope="minor">Corrections between the lines, in a
   browner ink.</handNote>
</handDesc>
<p>Si quis <add hand="#hand-B">episcopus</add> migrauerit, deponatur.</p>
<listRelation>
 <relation name="scribe" type="judgement"
  cert="medium" active="#hand-A"
  passive="#new_person_exemplus_scriba"/>
</listRelation>

A hand pointed to that the file doesn't describe is an error, and a script or an execution outside the seeds is reported:

Breaks hand-declared:

<p>Si quis <add hand="#hand-B">episcopus</add>
<!-- breaks the rule --> deponatur.</p>

Breaks script-term:

<handDesc>
 <handNote xml:id="hand-carolingian"
  script="carolingian">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
  <p>The main hand.</p>
 </handNote>
</handDesc>

Breaks execution-term:

<handDesc>
 <handNote xml:id="hand-calligraphic"
  script="minusculeCaroline" execution="calligraphic">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
  <p>The main hand.</p>
 </handNote>
</handDesc>

VIII.6 Decoration

A unit's decoration is its decoDesc's decoNotes, each of a kind, in type from the vocabulary of decoration (colInit for coloured initials, rubrication, miniature, …), with its folios and prose. A made-up unit:

<decoDesc>
 <decoNote type="colInit">
  <locus from="1r" to="40v">ff. 1r–40v</locus>: red and green initials at the
   chapters.</decoNote>
 <decoNote type="rubrication">Rubrics in red.</decoNote>
</decoDesc>

A kind outside the seeds is reported:

Breaks decoration-term:

<decoDesc>
 <decoNote type="initials">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
  <p>Red initials.</p>
 </decoNote>
</decoDesc>

VIII.7 The binding

The binding is the manuscript's, in its own physical description, which holds nothing else: a bindingDesc with a binding, described in paragraphs. Oxford encoding guide: "The standard dating attributes should be used on the binding element if information about the date of the binding is available." Its bounds go on binding, in the one form of dates (VII Dates), and where they aren't known, contemporary says whether it is the manuscript's first binding: true, false, or unknown where nothing is known. The binding's materials and techniques are terms in its prose, each pointing by ref to its concept in Ligatus's Language of Bindings; its dimensions, with depth beside height and width, stand in its prose too. An earlier binding of one unit, before it was joined to the others, is said in a paragraph of the unit's physical description. A made-up binding:

<bindingDesc>
 <binding notBefore="1450" notAfter="1500"
  contemporary="false">
  <p>
   <term ref="http://w3id.org/lob/concept/1238">Calfskin</term> over wooden boards, blind-tooled; two clasps,
     lost. <dimensions type="binding" unit="mm">
    <height>250</height>
    <width>175</width>
    <depth>60</depth>
   </dimensions>
  </p>
 </binding>
</bindingDesc>

A binding neither dated nor said contemporary is an error, and so is a value of contemporary outside the three:

Breaks binding-dated:

<binding>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 <p>Calf over boards.</p>
</binding>

Breaks binding-contemporary:

<binding contemporary="inapplicable">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 <p>Calf over boards.</p>
</binding>
Element bindingchange

VIII.8 History

A unit's history (history) is its origin and its owners; the manuscript's, the owners of the whole book since its units were joined, and where it was assembled. The origin (origin) holds the unit's date, origDate, in the one form of dates, its calendar named (VII Dates), and its place, origPlace, pointing to the place's record by ref; the import makes the relation origin from them. Each owner is a provenance, or an acquisition for the library that holds the manuscript now, with its dates and the owner named inside by persName, orgName or placeName with ref, since TEI gives neither a pointer of its own; the import makes the relation ownedBy. Oxford encoding guide: "where possible each provenance event should occur in its own provenance element, which should have relevant dating attributes (when, notBefore, etc.)." Clm 4592's third unit was made at Salzburg in the second quarter of the ninth century:

<origin>
 <origPlace ref="#new_place_salzburg">Salzburg</origPlace>
 <origDate notBefore="0826" notAfter="0850"
  calendar="#gregorian">sec. IX secondo quarto</origDate>
</origin>

A made-up owner, with the years of its ownership:

<provenance notBefore="1460"
 notAfter="1520">Owned by <orgName ref="#new_org_ecclesia_exemplensis">the cathedral
   of Exemplum</orgName>, whose ex libris stands on f. 1r.</provenance>

An origDate has no type but undated (VIII.2 Units):

Breaks orig-date-type:

<origDate type="circa" notBefore="0776"
 notAfter="0825" calendar="#gregorian">ca. 800</origDate>
<!-- breaks the rule -->

VIII.9 Images, literature, sources

What a description adds about the manuscript goes in the manuscript's additional: the description's own sources, in adminInfo's recordHist, each a bibl typed source in source, as every record names its own (V.11 Sources); the manuscript's images, in surrogates, each IIIF manifest a ref typed iiif, its address in target and its provider in its words, which the application shows the pages from (a page break's canvas is the page break's, III.9.2 Page breaks); and the literature on it, in listBibl, in the forms of Part IX. A made-up manuscript's:

<additional>
 <adminInfo>
  <recordHist>
   <source>
    <bibl type="source">
     <title>Mirabile</title>
     <idno type="mirabile">123456</idno>
     <date type="retrieved"
      when="2026-11-04">4 November 2026</date>
    </bibl>
   </source>
  </recordHist>
 </adminInfo>
 <surrogates>
  <ref type="iiif"
   target="https://example.org/iiif/can-12/manifest.json">Bibliotheca Exemplensis</ref>
 </surrogates>
 <listBibl>
  <bibl>
   <ptr target="#new_bibl_catalogue_exemplensis"/>
   <citedRange unit="page" from="12" to="13">12–13</citedRange>
  </bibl>
 </listBibl>
</additional>

VIII.10 The Bodleian's descriptions

The Bodleian Libraries' catalogues, and others written in their form, describe manuscripts in TEI with a schema of their own, which this part's description is modelled on. Their descriptions are an import format: the project converts them into its own form, with a converter outside this package. They needn't pass this guide's schema; before they are converted, they are checked against their own, at the commit the package pins:

solemne-tei validate --format bodleian description.xml⚓

The table in Appendix E The import formats says what the conversion does with each element and attribute of the Bodleian's description: keeps it, moves it, makes a record or a relation of it, or drops it, and why. Of the project's coded files, 38 carry a description in the Bodleian's form, written before this guide; the corrections bring them to the guide's form.
