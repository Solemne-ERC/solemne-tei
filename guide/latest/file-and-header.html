The SOLEMNE Encoding Guidelines

II The file and its header

A file is one document: a TEI root, TEI, in TEI's namespace, holding a header, teiHeader, the text, text, and, where the file lists records, relations or annotations, a standOff after the text. The header says what the document is, who made the file and from what, and how the text was encoded. Its skeleton is required: the title statement with its formal title, the publication statement, and the description of the source. The rest is common or full, as each section says.

The root needs no id: the document's code, in the formal title, identifies it. A root's xml:id is the encoder's, and the import doesn't read it.

II.1 The title statement

The title statement (titleStmt) opens with the formal title, title typed formal, which holds the document's identifiers, once each:

  • its code, an idno without a type (II.1.2 The coding rules);
  • its category, an idno typed category;
  • its type, an idno typed type.

Its other titles follow: an alias, the title readers know it by, and titles typed other, each naming its source in source. The formal title and its three identifiers are required: the import needs the code for the document's id. The Schematron checks them as errors, which the import reports. An alias is common; other titles are full. The Council of Mainz, 813:

<titleStmt>
 <title type="formal">
  <idno>C.MAI-0813</idno>
  <idno type="category">C</idno>
  <idno type="type">edition</idno>
 </title>
 <title type="alias">Council of Mainz, 813</title>
</titleStmt>

Breaks title-first-formal:

<titleStmt>
 <title type="alias">Council of Samplia, 600</title>
 <title type="formal">
  <idno>C.SAM-0600</idno>
  <idno type="category">C</idno>
  <idno type="type">edition</idno>
 </title>
</titleStmt>

Breaks title-one-formal:

<titleStmt>
 <title type="formal">
  <idno>C.SAM-0600</idno>
  <idno type="category">C</idno>
  <idno type="type">edition</idno>
 </title>
 <title type="formal">Council of Samplia</title>

<title type="formal">
 <idno>C.SAM-0600</idno>
 <idno type="category">C</idno>
</title>

Identifiers in the title statement stand in the formal title, and are its code, its category, its type, or, in an export, the permalinks of the levels the document belongs to: one each for its work, its version, its witness and its transcription (I.2 The levels of a text). Those are export only: the application writes them.

</title>

II.1.1 Categories and types

The category says what kind of text the document is, or, for W, how the work is known. The categories are a closed list, released with the guide: a category the application adds comes to the guide first and joins it in a release, with its prefix, since every code's and every pointer's prefix depends on it.

  • SYSCOLL: a collection arranged by subject;
  • CHRCOLL: a collection arranged chronologically;
  • FARCOLL: a collection in no order, a farrago, as Clavis canonum calls it;
  • C: a council;
  • D: a decretal;
  • P: a penitential;
  • H: a homiliary;
  • EX: exegesis;
  • EP: a letter, not a pope's;
  • LR: Roman law, lex Romana;
  • CPT: a capitulary;
  • CHA: a charter;
  • BIB: the Bible;
  • PATR: a patristic work not known by a siglum;
  • SCHOL: a scholarly work worth citing into, such as Kéry's catalogue of canonical collections;
  • W: a work known by its siglum in Gryson's Répertoire général des auteurs ecclésiastiques latins, whatever its kind: a Father's letter cited by its siglum is W, not EP;
  • UNK: not known.

The type says what the file transcribes:

  • edition: a text as a scholarly edition prints it;
  • manuscript: a text as a manuscript gives it, transcribed from it; its source description is the manuscript's (VIII Manuscripts);
  • reconstruction: a text that scholars have reconstructed from others, such as a collection known from parts of other collections;
  • hypothesis: any text supposed rather than found, less precise than a reconstruction;
  • placeholder: a text whose content stands in for another's, such as a manuscript's text taken for now from the edition that used it;
  • register: not a document but a list of records, with no text: its formal title holds its type alone, without a code or a category (V.13 Registers).

Breaks category-value:

<title type="formal">
 <idno>CAN.SAM-0600</idno>
 <idno type="category">CAN</idno>
 <idno type="type">edition</idno>
</title>

Breaks document-type-value:

<title type="formal">
 <idno>C.SAM-0600</idno>
 <idno type="category">C</idno>
 <idno type="type">copy</idno>
</title>

Breaks type-manuscript:

<title type="formal">
 <idno>C.SAM-0600</idno>
 <idno type="category">C</idno>
 <idno type="type">manuscript</idno>
</title>

II.1.2 The coding rules

A document's code says what the document is: its category, its title, its date, and, where needed, its source. Codes are made by the rules below, from the old Solemne guide, revised. The Schematron checks the code's form and its prefix, that a manuscript's code goes with the manuscript's description, and that a file named by a code is named by its own; the rest of the rules are the project's practice, explained here and not checked. A tool that proposes codes may come later.

A code has up to three parts, joined by dots:

  • The category's prefix: the category itself (C, D, P, H, EX, EP, LR, CPT, CHA, BIB, PATR, SCHOL, W), and COLL for the three kinds of collection. A document of an unknown category takes any prefix.
  • The title part, its subparts joined by hyphens:
    • the title: for a council, three capitals for its place (C.MAI for Mainz); for a decretal, three for its pope (D.LEO), with Ps before a forged decretal's pope (D.PsCAL); for a collection or a penitential, its name or an abbreviation of it (COLL.Dach, COLL.400, P.HAL); for a code of Roman law, the abbreviation its editions cite it by (LR.CTh for the Codex Theodosianus, LR.CJ for Justinian's Codex, LR.Inst, LR.Dig); for a capitulary, a bishop's statutes or a charter, three letters for the person who issued it (CPT.CHL for Chlothar II, CPT.GER for Gerbald of Liège, CHA.RAT for Ratherius of Verona); for the Bible, its version (BIB.Vulg, the Vulgate); for a work known by its Gryson siglum, the siglum (W.IS-ety, below); UNK where the title isn't known;
    • the date, where wanted: four digits, the year (C.MAI-0813), and for a code of Roman law the year of its promulgation (LR.CTh-0438, LR.CJ-0534); the latest possible year, where the date is a range; 9999 where the date isn't known (EP.LupEuf-9999);
    • a further identifier, where needed: a decretal's number in Jaffé's register, in Kaltenbrunner's numbering (D.LEO-0447-414); a number, where one issuer and one year have several texts, in the edition's order (CPT.KAR-0813-1 to -4, CHA.HEN-1002-1 to -12); a version, -v and a name or the edition's letter (C.NIC-0325-vDIO1, Nicaea in the first Dionysiana; D.LEO-0446-410-v1; CPT.HLU-0816-vA, a version Mordek prints apart).
  • The source part, where the code would otherwise be another document's: ms- and the manuscript's short code, for every transcription of a manuscript (COLL.400.ms-Mun-BSB-4592); ed- and the edition, where the corpus holds two editions of one text: the volume of the Patrologia Latina (COLL.Burch.ed-PL140, CHA.RAT-0974.ed-PL136), SCTA's Gratian (COLL.Grat.ed-SCTA), a series and its volume (C.GRM-0742.ed-MGHCapit1, Karlmann's capitulary of 742 in the MGH's Capitularia beside the council in its Concilia).

A manuscript's short code joins, by hyphens, its city in three letters, its library's abbreviation and its shelfmark: Mun-BSB-4592 for Munich, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Clm 4592. A code is a valid xml:id: ASCII letters and digits joined by dots and hyphens, without spaces, accents or parentheses, so a former shelfmark has no place in it. An underscore stands only in a pointer, before a citation (IV.4 Sources and the pointers' forms).

A law of a Roman code is cited by its book, title and law, as the editions cite it: "CTh 16.2.47" is LR.CTh-0438_16.2.47, its divisions numbered so (III.6 Prefaces, closers and subscriptions).

Each category has its own title part, and those for texts a person issues name the issuer: a decretal its pope, a capitulary, a bishop's statutes and a charter the one who issued them. One name takes one set of letters, whoever bears it, the year telling them apart, as D.LEO names every Leo: Charlemagne, Charles the Bald and Charles III are all KAR (CPT.KAR-0813-2, CPT.KAR-0869, CPT.KAR-0880). An issuer the edition leaves open between two, or doesn't name, is UNK, and a statute without a name of its own has the edition's name for it in its alias. A capitulary's year is the latest its edition's heading gives ("a. 805/813" is 813), a charter's too. A collection of capitularies is a collection (COLL.Ansegis). A letter, a homiliary, exegesis, a patristic work and a scholarly work take their title parts with their first files; until then, the Schematron checks their codes' form and prefix.

A work known by its siglum in Gryson's Répertoire takes the siglum as its title part, its spaces, dots and underscores made hyphens, the author's hyphen kept, and no date: W.IS-ety for Isidore's Etymologiae, W.HI-ep-52 for Jerome's letter 52, W.AU-s-351 for Augustine's sermon 351. A file holds one text, as the siglum's work part names it: a treatise is one file, and a numbered collection of texts, such as a Father's letters or sermons, one file for each, its number in the work part (HI_ep.52). It is cited by its divisions, as any code is: Isidore's book 15, chapter 2, section 1 is W.IS-ety_15.2.1, and a pointer by the siglum, IS_ety_15.2.1, names the same place (IV.4 Sources and the pointers' forms). A work that has a code of its own, such as a pope's letter, keeps it, and its siglum stands on its record (V.6 Works and versions). A made-up file of the Etymologiae opens so:

<titleStmt>
 <title type="formal">
  <idno>W.IS-ety</idno>
  <idno type="category">W</idno>
  <idno type="type">edition</idno>
 </title>
 <title type="alias">Isidore of Seville, Etymologiae</title>
</titleStmt>

A capitulary's chapter is cited by its number, as its edition numbers it: CPT.CHL-0614_9. Ansegis's collection, by book and chapter: COLL.Ansegis_2.5. A charter is cited whole, by its code, or by its own numbers where it numbers its parts (CHA.RAT-0974.ed-PL136_2). Chlothar II's edict of 614 opens so:

<titleStmt>
 <title type="formal">
  <idno>CPT.CHL-0614</idno>
  <idno type="category">CPT</idno>
  <idno type="type">edition</idno>
 </title>
 <title type="alias">Chlotharii II. edictum, 614 (BK 9)</title>
</titleStmt>

Ratherius of Verona's testament, in Migne's text:

<titleStmt>
 <title type="formal">
  <idno>CHA.RAT-0974.ed-PL136</idno>
  <idno type="category">CHA</idno>
  <idno type="type">edition</idno>
 </title>
 <title type="alias">Ratherius of Verona, Testamentum, in Migne's Patrologia Latina 136</title>
</titleStmt>

The Schematron checks the form: the prefix and its dot; the title part, whose subparts, the date among them, are joined by hyphens; and any source part after a dot, which begins with a letter. A code with an underscore fails, and so does a date joined by a dot (P.SAM.0700 for P.SAM-0700):

Breaks code-format:

<title type="formal">
 <idno>C.SAM_0600</idno>
 <idno type="category">C</idno>
 <idno type="type">edition</idno>
</title>

 <idno>P.SAM.0700</idno>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 <idno type="category">P</idno>
 <idno type="type">edition</idno>
</title>

 <idno>C.SAM-0600</idno>
 <idno type="category">D</idno>
 <idno type="type">edition</idno>
</title>

Breaks code-manuscript:

 <idno>COLL.Samp.ms-Now-NL-12</idno>
 <idno type="category">SYSCOLL</idno>
 <idno type="type">edition</idno>

A file named by a code, whatever the category's, is named by its own: C.MAI-0813.xml. A file named otherwise isn't checked; one named by another code is reported. Only a whole file can show it:

II.2 Responsibility and funding

After its titles, the title statement may name who did what for the document: its editors, the scholars whose study it rests on, the cataloguer of its manuscript, its encoders. Each is a respStmt with what they did, resp, dated by when where known, and who, a persName or an orgName pointing to its record in ref (IV.1 Names and their records). A funder names who paid for the work. Both are full. The Council of Carthage of 397 names its editor and its funder:

<respStmt>
 <resp when="1974">Editor</resp>
 <persName>Charles Munier</persName>
</respStmt>
<funder>
 <orgName>
  <ref target="https://erc.europa.eu/homepage">European Research Council</ref>
 </orgName>
</funder>

The header points to records as the text does, by ref, in the same forms: a title to its work, an author or an editor to a person. key and corresp keep TEI's other uses; a record's pointer in them is reported. provenance, acquisition and origin, in a manuscript's description, have no ref in TEI: the record they point to is named inside them, by a name with ref, and the import makes the relation from that name.

Breaks record-pointer-in-ref:

<sourceDesc>
 <bibl>
  <author key="#new_person_Exemplus">Exemplus</author>
  <title>Epistolae</title>
 </bibl>
</sourceDesc>

Breaks record-pointer-form:

<sourceDesc>
 <bibl>
  <author ref="new_person_Exemplus">Exemplus</author>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
  <title>Epistolae</title>
 </bibl>
</sourceDesc>

II.3 Publication and source

The publication statement (publicationStmt) says who publishes the file, not its source: for the project's files, the project, with its address. It's required, and may be a paragraph:

<publicationStmt>
 <publisher>SOLEMNE: 'The Social Life of Early Medieval Normative Texts'</publisher>
 <address>
  <orgName type="institution">Radboud University</orgName>
  <street>Houtlaan 4</street>
  <settlement>Nijmegen</settlement>
  <postCode>6525 XZ</postCode>
  <country>Netherlands</country>
 </address>
</publicationStmt>

The source description (sourceDesc), also required, describes what the text was transcribed from: an edition, as a biblStruct (IX Editions and literature); a manuscript, as a manuscript description (VIII Manuscripts); for a reconstruction, the editor's publication, with the witnesses it rests on in a listWit. The Council of Mainz, 813, from Werminghoff's edition:

<sourceDesc>
 <biblStruct>
  <analytic>
   <title level="a">Concilium Moguntinense. 813. (mense Maio) - Iun. 9.</title>
  </analytic>
  <monogr>
   <editor>
    <persName>
     <forename>Albert</forename>
     <surname>Werminghoff</surname>
    </persName>
   </editor>
   <title level="m">Concilia aevi Karolini</title>
   <imprint>
    <pubPlace>Hannover</pubPlace>
    <date>1906</date>
   </imprint>
   <biblScope unit="page">258-273</biblScope>
  </monogr>
  <series>
   <title level="s">Monumenta Germaniae Historica</title>
   <biblScope unit="volume">2.1</biblScope>
  </series>
 </biblStruct>
</sourceDesc>

Breaks authority-term:

<sourceDesc>
 <bibl>
  <title>Concilia exempli gratia</title>
  <idno type="isbn">0-000-00000-0</idno>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 </bibl>
</sourceDesc>

A file taken from another project's names that project as its source, in a bibl typed source after the witness's description, which stays whole: the description says what the text is, the source where and when this file took it, in the form a record's source has (V.11 Sources); a register names every list it's made from (V.13 Registers). Its name, in title, is required; then a link to the text there (ref), the date it was taken (date typed retrieved, its day in when), the licence it was taken under, where the source states one (licence, in availability), the source's version or release, where it has one (edition), and a credit the source gives, in its own words, in a note. The text's id there is on the text, in sameAs (II.4.3 Other projects' ids). A file has one source at most, and one made from an edition or a manuscript by the project's encoders names none. All of it is full. Abbo of Fleury's Canones, Migne's text taken from Corpus Corporum:

<sourceDesc>
 <biblStruct>
  <analytic>
   <author>Abbo Floriacensis; Robertus Francorum</author>
   <title level="a">Canones</title>
  </analytic>
  <monogr>
   <editor>
    <persName>
     <forename>Jacques-Paul</forename>
     <surname>Migne</surname>
    </persName>
   </editor>
   <title level="m">Patrologiae cursus completus. Series latina</title>
   <imprint>
    <publisher>J.-P. Migne</publisher>
    <pubPlace>Paris</pubPlace>
    <date>1853</date>
   </imprint>
   <biblScope unit="volume">139</biblScope>
   <biblScope unit="column" from="473A"
    to="508A">473A–508A</biblScope>
  </monogr>
 </biblStruct>
 <bibl type="source">
  <title>Corpus Corporum</title>
  <ref target="https://mlat.uzh.ch/browser/cps_2.AbFlHuF.Canone5">mlat.uzh.ch</ref>
  <date type="retrieved" when="2026-11-06">6 November 2026</date>
  <note>this file was encoded in TEI xml for the University of Zurich's Corpus Corporum project
     (www.mlat.uzh.ch) by Ph. Roelli in 2019</note>
 </bibl>
</sourceDesc>

A text edited on a website, which is both its edition and its source, is named in both, for two things: the biblStruct describes the edition, with the site's address in an idno typed url; the source says when this file took it.

The import keeps the source as the text's provenance: the source's name, the link, the date retrieved, the licence and the version, and the text's place there, from the link or from sameAs; the credit stays with the source. A file without a source is given one at import, with the set it comes in. This reading is for the application to confirm.

A file has one source, every source has a name, and a source with a link gives the date it was taken, since the link may lead to a text that has changed since:

Breaks text-source-one:

<sourceDesc>
 <bibl>
  <title>Canones exempli gratia</title>
 </bibl>

  <date type="retrieved" when="2026-11-06">6 November 2026</date>
 </bibl>
 <bibl type="source">
<!-- breaks the rule: a second source -->
  <title>Alterum exemplum</title>
  <date type="retrieved" when="2026-11-07">7 November 2026</date>

 </bibl>
 <bibl type="source">
<!-- breaks the rule: no name -->

  <date type="retrieved" when="2026-11-06">6 November 2026</date>

II.4 How the text was encoded

The encoding description (encodingDesc) says, once for the file, what the text's encoding needs explained. All of it is full. In its editorial declaration (editorialDecl), a normalization says what the transcription normalised (III.10.4 Abbreviations, spelling, punctuation); the sections below give the rest.

II.4.1 Inferred units

Where the encoder didn't mark the units of reception (III.4 Units), so that a division's paragraphs may be several units, a segmentation typed inferred-units says so, with a paragraph, which TEI requires. For the whole file, it is the default (default true) or stands alone. For a few divisions among many marked units, it has default false and an id, and those divisions point to it in decls; one that none points to is reported.

<editorialDecl>
 <segmentation xml:id="units-inferred-here"
  type="inferred-units" default="false">
  <p>The units of reception aren't marked in the chapters that point here: each paragraph may be one.</p>
 </segmentation>
</editorialDecl>
<div type="chapter" n="7"
 decls="#units-inferred-here">
 <p>…</p>
 <p>…</p>
</div>

Breaks inferred-units-pointed-to:

<editorialDecl>
 <segmentation xml:id="inferred-in-some-chapters"
  type="inferred-units" default="false">
  <p>The units of reception aren't marked in some chapters: each is one.</p>
 </segmentation>
</editorialDecl>

Breaks segmentation-type-unknown:

<editorialDecl>
 <segmentation type="units-inferred">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
  <p>Units inferred.</p>
 </segmentation>
</editorialDecl>

II.4.2 Curation

How far a text has been checked is its curation: correction, in the editorial declaration, with its status, high, medium, low or unknown, and a paragraph saying how, which TEI requires. The whole text's is the default; a division or a paragraph whose text was checked otherwise points to another correction in decls. Of several, one is the default. A text's completeness is never stated: the application counts it from the gaps.

<editorialDecl>
 <correction xml:id="checked-text"
  status="high" default="true">
  <p>Checked against the edition.</p>
 </correction>
 <correction xml:id="unchecked-chapter"
  status="low">
  <p>Not checked yet.</p>
 </correction>
</editorialDecl>
<div type="chapter" n="12"
 decls="#unchecked-chapter">
 <p>…</p>
</div>

A file that states no curation is imported as medium, checked at least once, when it is one of the project's coded files, a file of the Patrologia Latina or SCTA's Gratian, and as low when it comes from handwritten text recognition. high is set in the application, node by node. What was left out by choice is said once, in samplingDecl.

Breaks correction-default:

<editorialDecl>
 <correction xml:id="checked-against-the-edition"
  status="high">
  <p>Checked against the edition.</p>
 </correction>
 <correction xml:id="not-checked-yet"
  status="low">
  <p>Not checked yet.</p>
 </correction>
</editorialDecl>

Breaks decls-target:

<editorialDecl>
 <correction xml:id="checked" status="high">
  <p>Checked against the edition.</p>
 </correction>
</editorialDecl>
<div type="chapter" n="1" decls="#unchecked">
 <p>Placuit ut episcopi in ciuitatibus suis maneant.</p>
</div>

II.4.3 Other projects' ids

A node's ids in other projects, SCTA's resources, the Clavis canonum's keys, Corpus Corporum's sections, go in sameAs, on the division or the paragraph, each with a prefix declared in the header by a prefixDef in listPrefixDef, which turns it into an address. The application knows four:

  • scta, SCTA: http://scta.info/resource/$1;
  • clavis, the Clavis canonum: https://data.mgh.de/databases/clavis/db/legacysearch?key=$1;
  • corpuscorporum, Corpus Corporum's Patrologia Latina: https://mlat.uzh.ch/browser/$1;
  • pseudoisidor, the Pseudoisidor project, whose address the guide gives when a file first carries its ids.

Any other prefix may be declared, and is reported; a prefix used without its declaration is an error. A Clavis key on a chapter:

<listPrefixDef>
 <prefixDef ident="clavis"
  matchPattern="(.+)"
  replacementPattern="https://data.mgh.de/databases/clavis/db/legacysearch?key=$1"/>
</listPrefixDef>
<div type="chapter" n="27"
 sameAs="clavis:IS35.27">
 <p>…</p>
</div>

A text of the Patrologia Latina has Corpus Corporum's id, cps_2. (its corpus 2, the Patrologia Latina) and the text's id there; a paragraph adds :, its division's position, ; and its position among the division's children, counted from 1 with the heading. For Absalon of Lund's Testamentum, PL 209, the text is corpuscorporum:cps_2.AbsLun.Testam17 and a paragraph corpuscorporum:cps_2.AbsLun.Testam17:3;16. The converter from PL's files writes them, on the text and on each paragraph that is one of the application's nodes; an encoder doesn't.

<listPrefixDef>
 <prefixDef ident="corpuscorporum"
  matchPattern="(.+)"
  replacementPattern="https://mlat.uzh.ch/browser/$1"/>
</listPrefixDef>
<p sameAs="corpuscorporum:cps_2.AbsLun.Testam17:3;16">…</p>

The positions are Corpus Corporum's, counted in its text as its site shows it, not in the file, whose divisions and headings may differ. A division is numbered by its position among its sibling divisions, one inside another adding its own after a dot (2.1); a paragraph by its position among its division's children, the heading counted, a page break not. In Isaac of Langres's Canones, PL 124, Corpus Corporum counts the preface as the first division, so that the first section is its second, and gives the section's first chapter one heading where the file has three: the chapter's paragraph is 2.1;2.

<listPrefixDef>
 <prefixDef ident="corpuscorporum"
  matchPattern="(.+)"
  replacementPattern="https://mlat.uzh.ch/browser/$1"/>
</listPrefixDef>
<div type="preface">
 <head type="rubric">ISAACI PRAEFATIO.</head>
 <pb n="1075C"/>
 <p sameAs="corpuscorporum:cps_2.IsaLin.Canone3:1;2">Cum opportunitas ecclesiastica exegerit …</p>
</div>
<div type="section" n="1">
 <head type="number">TITULUS PRIMUS.</head>
 <head type="rubric">DE POENITENTIBUS.</head>
 <div type="chapter" n="1">
  <head type="number">CAP. I.</head>
  <head type="rubric">De poenitentibus non cogendis vinum bibere.</head>
  <head type="inscription">Ex libro V Capitularis, cap. 54.</head>
  <p sameAs="corpuscorporum:cps_2.IsaLin.Canone3:2.1;2">
   <pb n="1077C"/>Ut nullus presbyter, aut laicus
     …</p>
 </div>
</div>

Breaks same-as-prefix-declared:

<div type="chapter" n="1"
 sameAs="clavis:IS01.01">
 <head type="number">I.</head>
 <head type="rubric">De episcopis.</head>
 <p>Placuit ut episcopi in ciuitatibus suis maneant.</p>
</div>

Breaks prefix-known:

<listPrefixDef>
 <prefixDef ident="unknown"
  matchPattern="(.+)"
  replacementPattern="https://example.org/$1">
  <p>An identifier of a project the application doesn't know.</p>
 </prefixDef>
</listPrefixDef>

A record's or a manuscript's ids in other catalogues aren't pointers but identifiers: idnos typed by their authority, its key in lower camel case (V.10 Ids elsewhere). The guide lists the authorities the application knows, and the list grows: a key it doesn't list is reported, not refused, unless the file declares it. A file declares an authority in the encoding description's classDecl, as an export declares a vocabulary's terms (X.6 The vocabularies): a taxonomy with the id authorities, and in it a category for each authority, its xml:id the key, its name in catDesc and, after the name, its links, each a ptr whose target holds $1 where the id goes. Where the link depends on the kind of record, as Mirabile's does, each link is typed by the kind's word, the one a new record's id has (person, place, org, event, bibl, work, version, group, manuscript). An authority may have no link. The import makes a declared authority it doesn't hold; one it holds under another key is the application's to map. The authorities the guide lists need no declaration. Mirabile's indices, and a catalogue made up for the example:

<classDecl>
 <taxonomy xml:id="authorities">
  <category xml:id="mirabile">
   <catDesc>Mirabile <ptr type="person"
     target="https://www.mirabileweb.it/author/-author/$1"/>
    <ptr type="work"
     target="https://www.mirabileweb.it/title/-/$1"/>
    <ptr type="version"
     target="https://www.mirabileweb.it/title/-/$1"/>
    <ptr type="manuscript"
     target="https://www.mirabileweb.it/manuscript/-manuscript/$1"/>
   </catDesc>
  </category>
  <category xml:id="catalogusExempli">
   <catDesc>Catalogus exempli gratia <ptr target="https://example.org/catalogus/$1"/>
   </catDesc>
  </category>
 </taxonomy>
</classDecl>

An authority's link holds $1, or it can't make a record's address:

Breaks authority-link:

<category xml:id="catalogusSineNumero">
 <catDesc>Catalogus sine numero <ptr target="https://example.org/catalogus/"/>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 </catDesc>
</category>

II.4.4 Calendars and vocabularies

The calendars and eras a file's dates use are declared once, and each date points to its own (VII Dates). An export declares the terms of the application's vocabularies it uses, in classDecl, each with its permalink (X Exports); a file may declare terms too, such as a segment's (IV.8 What no element fits).

II.5 The abstract

The profile description (profileDesc) may hold an abstract: what the document is, its context, and what the file leaves out of it. It's full, and the import keeps it as the document's description. The Council of Aachen of 836:

<abstract>
 <p>Note that edition also contains a preface to this council, which has been omitted here.</p>
</abstract>

II.6 The history of the file

The revision description (revisionDesc) lists the file's changes, the latest first, each a change with its date in when and who made it in who. The import keeps it as the file gives it, read-only, as the history before the import. An export writes the application's releases and a summary of the changes since the last, and, on request, each change set (X Exports). It's full. The Council of Carthage of 397:

<revisionDesc>
 <change when="2025-03-21">Initial XML-TEI encoding.</change>
</revisionDesc>
