The SOLEMNE Encoding Guidelines

X Exports

An export is what the application writes: a transcription, or a selection of it, with its structure, the annotations its reader may see, and the records it points to. It's written in the forms this guide gives for every file, so that an export imported again gives the same text and the same annotations, and it adds what only the application knows: its ids, its permalinks, its history and its statuses. This part says what an export adds, for anyone reading one. The elements that only exports write are export only: the citation structure, and text reuse; what an encoder may write too keeps its tier.

The template export.xml is a short export of the Council of Soissons of 744, as the application writes it; the examples below come from it.

X.1 Ids, permalinks and records

Every node has its xml:id, the id the application gave it when it was imported or made: the document's code, _, and the node's citation at that moment, with .h1 or .p1 for a heading or a paragraph (I.3 Codes and ids). A node keeps its id when it moves, so an id may no longer match the node's citation. The formal title gives the levels the file belongs to, each by its permalink (II.1 The title statement):

<div type="chapter" n="1"
 xml:id="C.SOI-0744_1">
 <head type="number"
  xml:id="C.SOI-0744_1.h1">
  <pb n="34"/>(I).</head>
 <p xml:id="C.SOI-0744_1.p1">In primitus constituimus fide catholica, quam constituerunt CCCXVIII episcopi in
 <eventName ref="https://example.org/id/event/EVENT.1">Niceno concilio</eventName>, ut denuntiaretur per
   universa regione nostra …</p>
</div>

A pointer to anything outside the file, a record, a canon, another document, is a permalink, since one code can name several kinds of thing (I.4 Citations and permalinks). The records an export points to are listed in its standOff, each with its own id as its xml:id, the permalink's last step, and its names, so that the file reads on its own. A record's links to pages that aren't an authority's record are written back in its note of links (V.10 Ids elsewhere), and every manuscript's date and every date in the text names its calendar (VII.2 Calendars and eras).

X.2 The citation structure

An export says how its nodes are cited, in the encoding description's refsDecl, by TEI's citeStructure: one for each kind of division at each depth the document has, its match selecting the divisions and its use giving each one's step of the citation, by the rules of I.4 Citations and permalinks. A chapter or a register item is cited by its number, in n (11a for one without a number after 11); a section by its number, or its position; the other divisions by a prefix and their position among their kind. Nested divisions nest their citeStructures, and their steps are joined by delim, a dot. The council's preface and its chapters:

<refsDecl>
 <citeStructure unit="preface"
  match="/TEI/text/body/div[@type = 'preface']"
  use="concat('pr', count(preceding-sibling::div[@type = 'preface']) + 1)"/>
 <citeStructure unit="chapter"
  match="/TEI/text/body/div[@type = 'chapter']" use="@n"/>
</refsDecl>

DTS reads the same structure. A version's canons have their own citations; a transcription's chapters are joined to them by relations, not by its citation structure.

X.3 Annotations in the standOff

An export writes an annotation inline, in the element of its layer, where it nests in the others; where it would cross one, it writes it in the standOff's listAnnotation, as an annotation. An encoder may do the same, by anchors (IV.8.2 Anchors). A stand-off annotation has:

  • its target, in target: a span of a node's text, by TEI's string-range(), the node's id, where the span begins and how long it is (#string-range(C.SOI-0744_pr1.p1,26,137)), counted in characters of the node's text as the application holds it, its words without the markup and each run of spaces as one, from 0; or a node, an anchor or the range between two, by their ids. An annotation over several spans or nodes has several targets;
  • its author, in a respStmt naming them by permalink, and its licence, in a licence;
  • its body: a note holding the element of its layer, with the attributes it has inline and the span's words, which the import checks the target against; for a note's layers, a source note, a parallel, a reception note or an editorial remark, the note itself, typed by its layer. One form for each layer, inline or not, so that the import reads both alike. TEI's motivation isn't used: the body says the layer.

The council's date, from its year to the king's, crosses the name of Childeric, which runs on past it, so it stands in the standOff:

<p xml:id="C.SOI-0744_pr1.p1">
 <pb n="33"/>In Dei nomine trinitatis. Anno septingentesimo quadragesimo quarto ab
incarnatione Christi sub die VI. Nonas Martii et luna XIIII. in anno secundo <persName ref="https://example.org/id/person/PERS.31">Childerici regis Francorum</persName> ego <persName ref="https://example.org/id/person/PERS.32">Pippinus, dux et princeps Francorum</persName>. …
</p>
<listAnnotation>
 <annotation xml:id="annotation-date"
  target="#string-range(C.SOI-0744_pr1.p1,26,137)">
  <respStmt>
   <resp>dated</resp>
   <persName ref="https://example.org/id/person/PERS.12">An editor</persName>
  </respStmt>
  <licence target="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/">Creative Commons Attribution 4.0</licence>
  <note>
   <date when="0744-03-06"
    calendar="#julian">Anno septingentesimo quadragesimo quarto ab incarnatione
       Christi sub die VI. Nonas Martii et luna XIIII. in anno secundo Childerici regis</date>
  </note>
 </annotation>
</listAnnotation>

A target points into the file or to a permalink, and a body is one note, which holds the layer's element alone, or is typed by a note's layer:

Breaks annotation-target:

<listAnnotation>
 <annotation xml:id="annotation-lost"
  target="#string-range(nowhere,0,10)">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
  <note type="editorial">A remark.</note>
 </annotation>
</listAnnotation>

Breaks annotation-body:

<p xml:id="annotated">Placuit ut episcopi in ciuitatibus suis maneant.</p>
<listAnnotation>
 <annotation xml:id="annotation-words"
  target="#string-range(annotated,0,7)">
  <note>A remark, and <persName ref="#new_person_Primus">Primus</persName>.</note>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 </annotation>
</listAnnotation>

The relations an export writes, its nodes' observations and, on request, the judgements on its canons, are in its standOff's listRelation, in their one form (VI Relations).

X.4 Text reuse

Text reuse the project has accepted is a relation, "is a text of", with its evidence (VI.3 The levels' relations). The raw hits of a run of the reuse detection go into an export only on request, filtered as the export is: one linkGrp typed reuse for each run, pointing to the run by resp, and one link for each hit, its xml:id the hit's, with two targets: the passage in the file, and the passage in the other document, its permalink followed by the same pointer. Scores and lengths go in the CSV export of the same selection, under the same ids. A made-up hit:

<p xml:id="C.SOI-0744_2.p1">Propterea nos una cum consensu episcoporum sive sacerdotum seu servorum Dei …</p>
<linkGrp type="reuse"
 resp="https://example.org/id/run/3">
 <link xml:id="hit-41"
  target="#string-range(C.SOI-0744_2.p1,0,40) https://example.org/id/transcription/C.ASC-0756#string-range(C.ASC-0756_1.p1,0,40)"/>
</linkGrp>

A hit joins two passages:

Breaks reuse-link:

<p xml:id="reused">Placuit ut episcopi in ciuitatibus suis maneant.</p>
<linkGrp type="reuse"
 resp="https://example.org/id/run/1">
 <link xml:id="hit-1"
  target="#string-range(reused,0,7)"/>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
</linkGrp>

Breaks link-group-type:

<p xml:id="grouped">Placuit ut episcopi in ciuitatibus suis maneant.</p>
<linkGrp type="parallels">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 <link xml:id="hit-2"
  target="#string-range(grouped,0,7) https://example.org/id/node/C.NIC-0325_15.p1"/>
</linkGrp>

X.5 Statuses and history

The text's curation is in the encoding description's correction, as in any file (II.4.2 Curation); its completeness isn't stated, since the gaps give it. Its history is in the revisionDesc, the latest first:

  • the releases: each a change typed release, its name in n (2027.1), its date in when, and as its words a summary of the changes since the last release;
  • the change sets, on request: each a change without a type, its date, its author's permalink in who, the nodes it touched in target, and its message as its words. An earlier text of a node is pointed to by its permalink and version (…@t3), not copied.

The history a file had before its import stays as it was, read-only (II.6 The history of the file). The council's first release, and a change set before it:

<revisionDesc>
 <change type="release" n="2027.1"
  when="2027-06-30">The project's first release: the council checked against
   its edition, its persons, places and events identified.</change>
 <change when="2027-05-02"
  who="https://example.org/id/person/PERS.12" target="#C.SOI-0744_2.p1">A reading
   corrected against the edition.</change>
</revisionDesc>

A release has its name and its date, and a change is a release or a change set:

Breaks release-named:

<revisionDesc>
 <change type="release">A release.</change>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
</revisionDesc>

Breaks change-type:

<revisionDesc>
 <change type="correction"
  when="2027-05-02">A reading corrected.</change>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
</revisionDesc>
Element changechange

X.6 The vocabularies

An export declares the terms of the application's vocabularies it uses, in the encoding description's classDecl: a taxonomy for each vocabulary, and in it a category for each term, its xml:id the term's name, its labels in catDesc, one for each language, its permalink in sameAs, since the category is that term, and its equivalents elsewhere in corresp. An element that uses a term names it, as an attribute's value or by a pointer, as the guide's parts say. The council's relations use one term:

<classDecl>
 <taxonomy xml:id="relation-types">
  <category xml:id="isTextOf"
   sameAs="https://example.org/id/term/isTextOf">
   <catDesc xml:lang="en">is a text of</catDesc>
  </category>
 </taxonomy>
</classDecl>

X.7 Both checks

An export passes both checks, the RelaxNG and the Schematron, without an error, and declares the schema it was checked against by <?xml-model?>, as the templates do (Must and should, and the checks). The application's own tests check its exports so, against the release of this package it installs; what an export must hold beyond what every file may, they check too. The template export.xml passes both checks without a warning.
