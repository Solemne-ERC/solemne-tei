Appendix F The changes

What each release changed, and what may now fail in files that passed the release before it, as the changelog gives it. Generated from the changelog.

Appendix F.1 1.1.0 (2026-11-10)

An attribution where no words of the text name the source; Mirabile's indices as five authorities; and the forms settled since 1.0.0: a relation's type by where it starts, an author with no person record, a second text of one work in one edition, the work groups' types.

Appendix F.1.1 What changes for existing files

Nothing that passed 1.0.0 fails: the new errors apply only to forms 1.0.0 refused, and the RelaxNG takes more, not less. New warnings:

  • authority-term on mirabile: the single key is retired, since Mirabile numbers each index on its own and a person may be in two. Each id takes its index's key, which its record's kind gives: a person's mirabileAuthor, a work's or a version's mirabileWork, an institution's mirabilePlace, a manuscript's mirabileManuscript; an id in a record's source takes its record's. mirabilePerson, for the people Mirabile's manuscripts name, is new: no file has one yet. In the project's files: 315 ids in 26 files of the corrected corpus, and 195 in 9 of the data project's (189 in its three registers, 6 in manuscript descriptions).
  • link-authority reports a link to Mirabile's institutions or persons, to HMML's reading room or to Biblissima's records in a note of links, as it reports other authorities' records: they belong in idnos. None in the project's files.
  • work-group-term reports a work group's type outside the application's four.

What now passes:

  • a note typed attribution, in an inscription or directly in a division, its sources in @source, records among them by # and their id;
  • mirabileAuthor, mirabilePerson, mirabileWork, mirabileManuscript and mirabilePlace, without a declaration.

On the project's files: the corpus as received and as corrected, and the data project's sets, meet nothing new but the authority-term warnings above. The data project's 160 files of Clavis canonum's analyses, their 41,738 author groups rewritten from editorial notes into attributions, pass without a problem.

Appendix F.1.2 The schema

  • Attributions: note/@type takes attribution; a note's @source takes a record by # and its id besides the pointers' forms; attribution-source (its sources named, a record by its id), attribution-place (in an inscription or a division) and note-source-records (a record by # only in an attribution), errors; text-note-type, annotation-body and source-note-codes take attribution notes.
  • Mirabile: idno/@type's seeds lose mirabile and gain its five indices.
  • link-authority knows Mirabile's institutions and persons, HMML's reading room and Biblissima's records.
  • Work groups: work-group-term, a warning, with the types oneOrigin, derivedCollections, oneManuscript and projectGrouping.

Appendix F.1.3 The guide

  • Part IV: attributions where no words name the source, with an example and counter-examples; Part III's headings, Part I's layers and Part X's stand-off bodies point to them.
  • Part VI: a relation's type follows where it starts: an observation from a transcription's node, a judgement from a passage, a version or a work, none from a record. The examples that start at a record lose their type.
  • Part X: an account with no person record, a respStmt in the title statement (account.17) that @who and @resp point to; the export template shows one.
  • Part II: a second text of one work in one edition, its item's number after the edition (D.LEO-0449-423.ed-Schon-222); a declaration's example with a made-up index.
  • Part V: Mirabile's indices by key, a work's link title/-title/; the work groups' types.

Appendix F.2 1.0.0 (2026-11-09)

The first stable release: from it on, a file that passes fails only after a major version. It holds the records the application's database starts from: known and new records, the registers that seed it, authorities that grow, the types of persons and events, the application's relation types, work groups, a record's literature; a century as an interval; the Bible's file; the codes of capitularies and charters; the categories of collections in no order, the Fathers, scholarship and works known by their Gryson siglum; another edition's pages and numbering.

Appendix F.2.1 What changes for existing files

Files that passed may now meet Schematron errors, which the import reports and --strict fails on:

  • record-id: a record listed in the standOff whose id is neither its kind's known id (PERS.12, PLACE.7, INST.3, EVENT.4, PUBL.5, GROUP.2, a work's code, a manuscript's short code) nor a new one, new_, its kind's word and a name (new_person_…, new_place_…). solemne_person_7 and a capital New_place_… fail; so does a person listed as new_place_…. In the project's corpus, 4 records in each copy, as received and as corrected.
  • event-typed: a new event, and every event of a register, has a type. In the project's corpus, the 213 events of each copy, which are new and have none; the registers give them their types.
  • register-form: a file without a text is a register, and a register has none.
  • book-code-section: a book of the Bible's code in @n stands only on a section.
  • authority-link: a declared authority's link holds $1 where the id goes.
  • edref-witness: a page break's or a milestone's @edRef names a witness listed in the file, by # and its id. No file of the project's corpus uses @edRef.

What now passes:

  • an idno typed by an authority the guide doesn't list: reported by a warning, authority-term, unless the file declares it, where it failed the RelaxNG before;
  • a register: a file of records with a header and a standOff, typed register in its formal title, without a code or a category;
  • an EDTF interval (0801/0900), a century's form;
  • a book's code (MAT) in a section's @n;
  • pointers with the prefix BIB, the Bible's file's;
  • the categories FARCOLL, PATR, SCHOL and W, and pointers with the prefixes PATR and SCHOL;
  • milestone, for another edition's numbering.

Warnings that change:

  • date-edtf-bounds compares a date with unspecified digits exactly: 09XX is 900 to 999, so with the bounds 901 to 1000 it's now reported; a century is 0901/1000. No file of the corpus writes an EDTF.
  • relation-term reports holdsOfficeIn, which hasRole replaces, and knows 41 more types, isLongerVersionOf among them.
  • person-term and event-term report a person's or an event's type outside the guide's seeds, and milestone-term a milestone's unit outside them.

On the project's files: the corpus as received and as corrected meets the 217 errors above in each copy and nothing else new; the corpus as received has 1,233 fewer RelaxNG errors, the formal title's identifiers of older types, which idno-type still reports. The data project's sets, its charters among them, pass as before.

Appendix F.2.2 The schema

  • Known and new records: a record's xml:id is its known id, the application's, or a new one, new_ and its kind's word; record-id checks both, for every kind, and for an edition or its volume in the header that has an id. A known record outside a register may hold its name alone; the rules that ask more of a record (person-main-name, event-typed, manuscript-unit) ask it of new records and of a register's. person-id goes into record-id.
  • Registers: the formal title's type register, holding nothing else (formal-title-idnos, document-type-value); register-form; text-source-one lets a register name every list it's made from; a note typed unreviewed says what wasn't checked.
  • Authorities that grow: idno/@type is open, its list of seeds a warning's (authority-term), with isil, doi, k10plus, dnb, openAlex, gryson, kery, whg and jaffe added, and permalink for an export's known records. A file declares an authority in a taxonomy authorities, a category for each, its links ptrs holding $1, typed by the record's kind where the link depends on it (ptr/@type gains the kinds' words).
  • Persons: person/@type, added: historical (the default), modernScholar, divine, fictitious, with person-term. personGrp joins the schema, for a group of persons.
  • Events: event/@type's seeds, council to royalAssembly, with event-term and event-typed.
  • Relations: 66 types to start with: the application's, from closeTranslationOf to firstPublishedIn, and seven between texts and collections (sameAs, correctionOf, closeRenderingOf, looseRenderingOf, paraphraseOf, reactionTo, replacedBy); isLongerVersionOf, SAWS's; hasRole in place of holdsOfficeIn.
  • A century: date-edtf-form takes an interval; date-edtf-bounds compares exactly.
  • The Bible: the category BIB, in the pointers' patterns and source-note-codes; div/@n takes the 73 books' codes, book-code-section keeping them on sections.
  • Categories: FARCOLL, a collection in no order, its codes under COLL; PATR, a patristic work; SCHOL, a scholarly work; W, a work known by its Gryson siglum; each in category-value, code-category, the pointers' pattern and source-note-codes. The list stays closed, released with the guide.
  • Another edition's numbering: milestone joins the schema, where another edition's unit begins, between divisions and paragraphs or inside them, with @unit (seeds book, chapter, canon, letter, section, and milestone-term), and @n and @edRef required; edref-witness for it and for pb.

Appendix F.2.3 The guide

  • Part V: known and new records, by kind; persons' types and groups; events' types; work groups as bibls typed workGroup, their members by memberOf; authorities that grow, a Jaffé number; a record known only from a list; registers, with a register of persons.
  • Part V, too: a record's literature, an untyped bibl in it; a work's record standing for its canonical version, which has the work's code; a work's Gryson siglum on its record.
  • Part VI: the 66 types with their SAWS terms; the types between units, narrower than isTextOf; roles by hasRole; an editor's order; versionOf's certainty and evidence.
  • Part VII: a century as an interval.
  • Part II: the coding rules for capitularies, bishops' statutes and charters (the issuer's letters and the year), for the Bible (BIB.Vulg), for a work known by its Gryson siglum (W.IS-ety), and an edition by its series and volume; the categories, closed; the register's type; declaring an authority.
  • Part III: a body headed by its own heading; the Bible's file, by book, chapter and verse, a book's prologue its preface; a charter's chrismon, monogram and seal, which stay text; another edition's pages and numbering, by page breaks and milestones naming it as a witness.
  • Part IV: a pointer to the Bible names its file's verse (MAT_18_19, MAT.18.19); a pointer by a Gryson siglum names one file and a place in it (HI_ep.52_14), or a work with its own code through its record.
  • Part I: the layer "another numbering".
  • Part X: an export's references to records point to the records it lists, each holding its permalink; a relation type's SAWS term in its category's corresp; a record's literature, and a work standing for its canonical version, as in a file.
  • Appendix D: the older forms these replace.
  • A template, register.xml.

Appendix F.4 0.8.0 (2026-11-08)

The first code of Roman law: how its code is made, and a law's principium with its id line.

Appendix F.4.1 What changes for existing files

Nothing that passed 0.7.0 fails: one rule asks less. A preface, a closer, subscriptions or a register with a number heading and no @n now passes number-heading-n. On the project's files, the coded corpus as received and as corrected, the counts are 0.7.0's, rule by rule.

Appendix F.4.2 The schema

  • number-heading-n asks for @n only on the divisions cited by their number: a preface, a closer, subscriptions and a register, cited by their prefix and position, keep a number heading without it, such as a law's principium with its id line ("CTh.16.2.47pr.").

Appendix F.4.3 The guide

  • Part II, the coding rules: a code of Roman law is the abbreviation its editions cite it by and the year of its promulgation (LR.CTh-0438, LR.CJ-0534); a law is cited by book, title and law (LR.CTh-0438_16.2.47). The capitularies' and charters' title parts still come with their first files.
  • Part III: a law's principium is its preface, cited pr1, its id line its number heading; the Breviary's interpretatio is a unit after its law's text. Two examples from the Codex Theodosianus, in The Latin Library's text.

Appendix F.6 0.6.0 (2026-11-07)

A text taken from another project names that project as its source, in its header.

Appendix F.6.1 What changes for existing files

The project's files that passed 0.5.0 still pass: on the 411 coded files, as received and as corrected, the counts are 0.5.0's, rule by rule. Two new Schematron errors could fail another file: two sources in one sourceDesc (text-source-one), or a source without its name (source-named). A source with a link and no date retrieved is reported (source-retrieved, a warning).

Appendix F.6.2 The schema

  • A text's source: a bibl typed source in sourceDesc, after the edition's or the manuscript's description, in the form a record's source has: the source's name in title, a link to the text there in ref, the date it was taken in a date typed retrieved, the licence and the source's version where known, and a credit in the source's own words in a note. At most one per file (text-source-one); every source, a record's too, has its name (source-named); one with a link gives the date it was taken (source-retrieved).

Appendix F.6.3 The guide

  • Part II, publication and source: a text's source, with Abbo of Fleury's Canones (Patrologia Latina 139) as Corpus Corporum gives them; a text edited on a website names the site both as its edition and as its source; what the import reads, still to be settled with the application. Parts I and V point to it.
  • Part II, other projects' ids: how Corpus Corporum numbers a paragraph: in its own text, not the file's, which may split its headings or number its divisions otherwise; a division inside another joined by a dot; page breaks not counted. An example from Isaac of Langres's Canones (Patrologia Latina 124).

Appendix F.7 0.5.0 (2026-11-06)

A note type for an edition's later collections, the later texts that took a passage over: reception.

Appendix F.7.1 What changes for existing files

Nothing that passed 0.4.0 fails: the new type widens a closed list. The warning for codes left in a note's words (source-note-codes) now checks reception notes and notes in the standOff too, reports older codes it missed (C.NIC_0325_11, an underscore for the hyphen), and no longer takes Gratian's abbreviations (D.5, C.16) for codes. On the project's 411 coded files, as received, this version reports 125,777 problems, against 0.4.0's 125,768: the 9 more are such older codes in source notes' words, all warnings. On the files as corrected, the count is the same as 0.4.0's.

Appendix F.7.2 The schema

  • Reception notes: a note typed reception gives the later texts that took the passage over, as an edition's tier of later collections lists them ("Burchard I, 135; Ivo, Decr. V, 234"): the editor's words, and the later texts' codes in @source. It stands inline, or in a stand-off annotation over its span, as source notes and parallels do: text-note-type and annotation-body take it. For this type, @source names the later texts, not the passage's sources.
  • Codes in a note's words: source-note-codes checks source and reception notes, inline and in the standOff. A word starting C. or D. counts as a code when a letter follows the dot, then a hyphen, an underscore or a dot, and a digit.

Appendix F.7.3 The guide

  • Part IV, notes: reception notes, and how to tell them from parallels, by the edition's form and not the dates; an example from the Collectio Dionysio-Hadriana of 1525, whose margins give Gratian's chapters, and a stand-off one. The pointers' forms name a reception note's @source.
  • Parts I, VI and X, appendix D: the reception note among the layers; how the import reads its codes, still to be settled with the application; the layer in stand-off bodies; older notes typed corresp that become reception notes.
  • Part VIII: the project converts the Bodleian's descriptions with a converter outside this package; the guide said a tool of this package would.

Appendix F.8 0.4.0 (2026-11-05)

The guide finished and published: Parts IX and X, on editions and on exports, with the export template; appendices C to F; the guide as HTML pages and as a PDF; the release workflow, which publishes the schema and the guide on GitHub Pages. 1.0.0 follows once the project's corrected files pass.

Appendix F.8.1 What changes for existing files

Files that passed 0.3.0 may now fail where a publication's volume or pages are in a unit outside the five, or a code joins its date by a dot. On the project's 411 coded files, as received, this version reports 125,768 problems, against 0.3.0's 125,685: the 83 more are the units of biblScope and citedRange in 61 files, all RelaxNG errors until the corrections rewrite them. The dotted codes of four penitentials (P.THB.0700 and three others) fail once the corrections move them to the formal title's code, unless the hyphen goes in with them.

  • A date in a code is joined by a hyphen: code-format refuses a date joined by a dot, as the coding rules have it (P.THB.0700 is P.THB-0700). A part of a code after a second dot is a source part, beginning with a letter (.ms-, .ed-).
  • Publications: the units of biblScope and citedRange are a closed list, volume, part, issue, page and column (scope-unit, cited-range-unit), so that each is a field of biblatex: a tome, vol, band, fascicle or heft now fails the RelaxNG. A title in a biblStruct has the level of its part, a in analytic, m or j in monogr, s in series (title-level, an error). A biblStruct's @type is biblatex's entry type, a warning outside biblatex's types (entry-type); @subtype goes.
  • Stand-off annotations: a target is an id in the file, a range, a span by string-range() or a permalink (annotation-target), and the body is one note, holding the layer's element alone or typed by a note's layer (annotation-body); both errors. annotation/@motivation goes: the body says the layer.
  • Exports' elements: a linkGrp is typed reuse or not at all (link-group-type), and a hit of text reuse has two targets (reuse-link); a change is typed release or not at all (change-type), and a release has @n and @when (release-named).

Appendix F.8.2 The guide

  • Part IX, editions and literature: a publication's parts and their titles' levels, the MGH case and texts with editors of their own, the two kinds of pages, the units, biblatex's entry types and the table of its fields, publications as records, and reconstructions.
  • Part X, exports: the nodes' ids, permalinks and the records listed; the citation structure; annotations in the standOff, each with its layer's element; text reuse; releases and change sets; the vocabularies' terms with their permalinks; both checks. The template export.xml is a short export of the Council of Soissons of 744.
  • Appendices C to F: C, the code tables and the vocabularies' seeds, each seed with where it comes from and its Oxford term; D, older encodings and what they became; E, the import formats' tables, the Bodleian's moved there from Part VIII; F, the changes, from this changelog. C and F are generated, as A and B are, into the HTML; the examples' sources follow them.
  • The guide's title: "The SOLEMNE Encoding Guidelines", on a title page with its authors, version, date and licences, in the HTML and the PDF.
  • The HTML, a page per part: solemne-tei docs --html writes a folder of pages, the title page and contents in index.html, a page for the front matter, each part and each appendix, and one per specification of TEI's reference, so that no page is large. The parts are numbered I to X; no page shows the build's date. The guide's stylesheets are in guide/style/, and the text is set in Junicode, served with the pages.
  • The PDF: solemne-tei docs --pdf makes the guide TEI Lite, then LaTeX, by TEI's stylesheets, and sets it with LuaLaTeX in Junicode, into build/pdf/solemne-guide.pdf. It leaves out TEI's reference for each element, which the HTML holds. Junicode is fetched once at a pinned release, checked by its SHA-256.

Appendix F.8.3 The package

  • The validator no longer hangs on a file whose publication fails beside its imprint, such as a volume in an older unit: libxml2 never finishes compiling imprint's pattern on its own, so the narrowing of a RelaxNG error to its element doesn't try it, and a failure inside an imprint is reported at its parent.
  • The release workflow: on a tag vX.Y.Z, it rebuilds the schema from an empty cache and checks it against the committed files, runs the tests, makes the HTML and the PDF, and publishes: a GitHub release with the schema's files, the HTML and the PDF, its notes this changelog's section; the pages, schema/X.Y.Z/, guide/X.Y.Z/ and latest/, keeping every earlier version, earlier releases' schemas included; from 1.0.0 on, the package on PyPI. On a pull request, or started by hand, it builds the same and publishes nothing.

Appendix F.9 0.3.0 (2026-11-05)

The guide for records, relations, dates and manuscripts: Parts V to VIII, with the schema's own manuscript description in place of the Bodleian's module, the Bodleian's descriptions as an import format, and two templates.

Appendix F.9.1 What changes for existing files

Files that passed 0.2.0 may now fail where they describe a manuscript, date by EDTF or name a calendar, or point to an authority's record from a note of links. On the project's 411 coded files, this version reports 125,685 problems, against 0.2.0's 125,126; the differences, by rule, are below.

  • Manuscript descriptions, our own: the schema takes TEI P5's manuscript description module, restricted to the guide's profile, in place of the Bodleian Libraries' consolidated schema. Descriptions that passed may now fail:
    • 21 of the module's elements, and the header's scriptNote, are left out, each said another way: accMat, catchwords, custEvent, custodialHist, dim, filiation, heraldry, material, msFrag, msItemStruct, musicNotation, objectType, scriptDesc, seal, sealDesc, secFol, signatures, stamp, typeDesc, typeNote, watermark; so are msItem/@class, handDesc/@hands, handNote's @scribe, @scribeRef, @scriptRef and @medium, and the Bodleian's additions (collation/@mainStructures, layout/@topLine, layout/@rulingMedium);
    • altIdentifier is typed former, siglum or code (alt-identifier-type); origDate/@type is undated or nothing (orig-date-type); binding/@contemporary is true, false or unknown (binding-contemporary);
    • new errors: inside a description, only the profile's elements, in their places, and the prose's locus, names, title, date, foreign, hi and ref (description-elements): contents and the physical description go on a unit, the manuscript's own physical description holds the binding alone, and num is left out; every unit is dated, or said undated by origDate type="undated" (unit-dated); dimensions give their unit (dimensions-unit); a binding is dated or says whether it's contemporary (binding-dated); a @hand pointing into the file points to a handNote (hand-declared);
    • new warnings: an item without its folios (item-locus); an object's form, a support's material, a hand's script and execution, and a decoration's type outside their vocabularies' seeds (form-term, material-term, script-term, execution-term, decoration-term);
    • the Bodleian's own rules go, among them its warnings on an item's language;
    • hi joins the schema, for italics and the like in a description's prose; handNote gains @execution; ref/@type gains iiif, for a manuscript's IIIF manifests in surrogates.

    On the corpus's 38 descriptions: 110 new errors of description-elements (60 num, 37 physical descriptions of the whole manuscript, 9 contents on the manuscript, 3 filiation, a unit's binding), 56 items without folios, 86 new RelaxNG errors (73 altIdentifiers, 7 msItem/@class, filiation), and the Bodleian's 35 warnings gone.

  • Dates, in one form: a date's bounds in TEI's W3C attributes, its EDTF in @when-custom, @from-custom and @to-custom with @datingMethod="#edtf". TEI's other forms of the same date are left out: the ISO attributes (@when-iso, …), @period, @datingPoint, @notBefore-custom and @notAfter-custom. No file of the corpus uses them.
  • Calendars declared: calendarDesc and calendar join the schema, and every @calendar and @datingMethod points to a declared calendar (calendar-declared, an error). Files that give calendar="Gregorian" without declaring it now fail: 46 dates in the corpus's 38 manuscript descriptions, which become calendar="#gregorian" with its declaration.
  • Calendars named: a manuscript's date (origDate) and a date in the text name the calendar of their words (date-calendar, an error): #gregorian for a modern statement such as "sec. IX", the source's own otherwise (a Julian day, an era). A unit said undated, and the dates of publications, records and the header, name none. The corpus's origDates all have one; no transcription marks a date yet.
  • Dates checked: no bound comes after its pair (date-bounds-order, an error; two dates in the corpus); an EDTF value has EDTF's form (date-edtf-form, an error); the bounds agree with the EDTF by years (date-edtf-bounds, a warning).
  • Relations: @cert on a relation is high, medium or low (relation-certainty, closed); a relation's @name outside the guide's types is a warning (relation-term). No file of the corpus has relations.
  • Pointers typed: ptr/@type is canonical or stand-in (a work's choices of versions) or via (what a text came through, in a relation), closed (pointer-type). No file of the corpus types a ptr.
  • A work's choices of versions: a work names at most one canonical version and one version that stands in for it (version-choices, an error).
  • Ids, not links, for an authority's records: a link into an authority's records (Mirabile's authors, titles and manuscripts, VIAF, GND, Wikidata, GeoNames, …) in a note of links is a warning (link-authority): its id goes in an idno typed by the authority. Notes of links keep the links to other pages. The corpus's 294 Mirabile links in notes of links are reported.
  • Extra fields: fs and f join the schema, for a record's fields that no element holds.

Appendix F.9.2 The package

  • Import formats: solemne-tei validate --format bodleian, and validate(paths, format="bodleian") and Validator(format="bodleian") from Python, check manuscript descriptions in the Bodleian's form against the Bodleian's own schema, which the project converts into its own. The package holds that schema at a pinned commit (7e8b89c): its RelaxNG as the Bodleian publishes it, and its Schematron compiled to XSLT, with its notice. Its rules are named as the Bodleian names them (origDate.check). FORMATS lists the formats.
  • The build no longer compiles the Bodleian's ODD for the schema, and the schema's generated files no longer carry its notice: they hold none of its material. It fetches the Bodleian's RelaxNG in place of its ODD.

Appendix F.9.3 The guide

  • Part V, records: placeholders, ids and permalinks; persons, places, institutions, events, works and versions, witnesses, work groups, rare kinds and their extra fields; ids elsewhere, with Mirabile's indices by the kind of record; sources. A work names its canonical version, and the version that stands in for it.
  • Part VI, relations: their form, the types to start with and their reverses, the levels' relations.
  • Part VII, dates: the one form, with the corpus's own wordings; calendars and eras, with the Julian day converted to the Gregorian in the bounds; what the checks require.
  • Part VIII, manuscripts: the description and the manuscript's identifier; units; contents, items inside items and an item's transcription; the physical description; hands, decoration, the binding; history; images, literature and sources; the Bodleian's descriptions as an import format, with the table of what the conversion does with each of their elements and attributes, in guide/mappings/. It is modelled on the Bodleian Libraries' guidelines for manuscript description, credited once in the front matter, and quotes them where their words fit.
  • A template for a collection in one manuscript, manuscript-collection.xml: the Collectio 400 capitulorum in Munich, Clm 4592, with the manuscript's description in the guide's form.
  • A template for a reconstruction, reconstruction.xml: the collection of Benedictus Levita, with the two editions it was put together from as its witnesses.

Appendix F.10 0.2.0 (2026-11-04)

The guide for texts: the front matter and Parts I to IV, the templates, and the guide as HTML. Encoders can use this version for texts; manuscripts, records, relations and dates come in the next.

Appendix F.10.1 What changes for existing files

Files that passed 0.1.0 may now fail where they have fragments, codes of the categories LR, CPT and CHA, or segments with terms. No file of the project's corpus has any of these yet: on its 411 coded files, this version reports the same problems as 0.1.0.

  • Fragments, two new errors: fragment-attributes: a later fragment (one with @prev) carries only its xml:id, @prev and @next, and the span's other attributes go on its first fragment; a segment's later fragments repeat its @type, which the RelaxNG requires, with the same value. fragment-order: @next points to an element later in the file, so that the fragments follow the text's order.
  • Codes: a code of the categories LR, CPT and CHA begins with LR., CPT. and CHA. (code-category), as the old guide's coding rules have it; it took any prefix before. UNK still takes any.
  • File names: code-matches-file-name reads any category's prefix in a file's name, not only C, D, COLL, P, EP, H and EX.
  • A segment's term: seg takes @ana, pointing to a category of a taxonomy in the header's classDecl; a term that isn't declared there is an error (seg-term-declared).

Appendix F.10.2 The guide

  • The ODD is split into the guide's parts: guide/solemne.odd is the root, and includes by XInclude one file per part in guide/chapters/, each holding its prose and the specifications of the rules it explains, which the schemaSpec takes by specGrpRef. Oxygen checks the root with its parts included.
  • The front matter and Parts I to IV are written: what a file describes; the file and its header, the coding rules among them; the text and its transcription; what is marked in the text. Each section has examples from the corpus, credited in a list of the examples' sources, and the counter-examples of its rules. Parts V to X hold their rules and counter-examples; their prose comes later.
  • Every element, attribute and rule names its tier, required, common, full or export only, by @corresp pointing to the tiers' taxonomy in the ODD's header; the elements taken from TEI unchanged take theirs from a table beside the specification. Each Schematron rule has a constraintSpec of its own, and each restriction the RelaxNG checks (a closed list, a required attribute, a pattern) an xml:id on the specification that makes it.
  • The guide's examples are tests: an example passes both checks, a counter-example (valid="false") fails on the rule its @n names, and every rule has one. Fragments are checked in a minimal file. The invalid samples that tested each rule are now counter-examples in the parts that explain their rules.
  • The templates, in guide/templates/, are whole files to start from, each a short text of the corpus: a minimal file, a rich one, a council and a collection from editions, a decretal and a penitential. Each declares the schema by two <?xml-model?> lines, at its version's address on GitHub Pages, and passes both checks.

Appendix F.10.3 The package

  • solemne-tei docs --html [--out DIR] writes the guide as one HTML page, by TEI's odd2html, with appendix A (the elements and attributes) and appendix B (every rule, with its check, severity, tier and part) generated from the specifications, and each counter-example labelled with the rule it breaks.
  • The build checks the ODD with its parts included, reports each error at its part's file and line, and writes the expanded ODD into the package as solemne.expanded.odd. The stamp in each generated file names the expanded ODD's SHA-256. It stops when a specGrpRef leads nowhere or a specGrp isn't referred to, which TEI's stylesheets would pass over without a word.

Appendix F.11 0.1.0 (2026-11-04)

The first release: SOLEMNE's TEI schema as a package.

Appendix F.11.1 The package

  • The schema's source is one ODD, guide/solemne.odd, under CC BY 4.0. The package holds what is generated from it: the RelaxNG, the Schematron and the Schematron compiled to XSLT, the compiled ODD, and closed-lists.json; each names the ODD's version and SHA-256.
  • solemne-tei validate <paths> [--jobs N] [--json] [--strict] checks files against the RelaxNG and the Schematron, in parallel; each problem has its file, line, check, severity, rule and message.
  • solemne-tei build [--cache DIR] regenerates the schema from the ODD in a clone, from pinned sources checked by their SHA-256; it says when nothing changed. It first checks the ODD against TEI's schema for ODD files, as Oxygen does, and stops if the ODD isn't valid.
  • solemne-tei lists [--json] prints the closed lists, the categories and the codes of the Bible's 73 books.
  • From Python: Validator, validate(paths, jobs), Problem, Result, closed_lists() and SCHEMA_VERSION. Validator also takes another schema's RelaxNG and compiled Schematron.

Appendix F.11.2 The forms

Files written for the draft of this schema need these changes to pass.

  • The formal title's identifiers: the document's code in an idno without a type, its category in idno type="category" and its type in idno type="type", once each; collCode, collCat and collType are no longer accepted. The code begins with its category's prefix (COLL for SYSCOLL and CHRCOLL); the categories gain LR, CPT and CHA, which take any prefix for now, as UNK does. A manuscript's code (.ms-) and the type manuscript go with the manuscript's description in the sourceDesc. An export adds there the permalinks of the levels the file belongs to, typed work, version, witness and transcription.
  • Pointers to records in the header: @ref, in the same forms as in the text (a placeholder #new_person_…, an id in the file, or a permalink), on title, author, editor, country, region, settlement, origPlace, repository, institution and the names. provenance, acquisition and origin name their record inside them, by a name with @ref. A record's pointer in @key or @corresp is reported, as a warning.
  • External ids: a node's ids in other projects go in @sameAs, each with a prefix declared in the header by prefixDef in listPrefixDef (clavis:IS35.27); an undeclared prefix is an error, and a prefix other than scta, clavis, pseudoisidor and corpuscorporum a warning. idno/@type is a closed list: the authorities (wikidata, viaf, gnd, geonames, tgn, pleiades, mirabile, clavis, biblissima, schoenberg, hmml, bodleian, manusOnline, the Bodleian's ieArk, crArk and SCN), the formal title's category and type, the levels' work, version, witness and transcription, and shelfmark, ISBN, url and biblatex.
  • Gaps: @reason is optional (without one, the reason is unknown) and closed: lacuna, damage, illegible, reproduction, deleted, sampling, editorial; the files' missing is lacuna. The extent, where known, is @quantity with @unit (leaves, lines, words, chars), which go together, or @extent.
  • The text's elements gain rs (a record of a kind no name element fits, its kind in @type, its record in @ref in the names' forms), anchor (a point a stand-off annotation refers to), supplied, and choice with abbr and expan, for the rare doubtful cases.
  • Curation: correction in editorialDecl, its @status high, medium, low or unknown, with a paragraph saying how, as TEI requires; the whole text's is the default, and a division or paragraph that differs points to another by @decls. Of several corrections, one is the default. @decls points only to a correction or a segmentation in the header.
  • Inferred units: segmentation type="inferred-units" states them for the whole file (@default true, or alone), or, with @default false and an id, for the divisions that point to it by @decls; a statement for divisions that none points to is a warning.
  • Manuscripts: a description (msDesc) has at least one codicological unit (msPart), with its own identifier.
  • Fragments: a span that crosses others or the structure, such as a quotation over several paragraphs, is split into fragments joined by @next and @prev; each points to the other within the file, on an element of the same kind.
