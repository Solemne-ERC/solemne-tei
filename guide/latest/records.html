The SOLEMNE Encoding Guidelines

V Records

A record is a thing the texts are about, or come from, which the application keeps once and every file points to: a person, a place, an institution, an event, a manuscript, a publication, a work and its versions. A file names records where its text names them (IV.1 Names and their records), and may list them, with what it knows of them, in its standOff: persons in listPerson, places in listPlace, institutions in listOrg, events in listEvent, works, versions and publications in listBibl. Every record may have its names, its dates in the one form (VII Dates), its ids elsewhere, its sources, notes, and its relations to other records (VI Relations). Manuscripts have a part of their own (VIII Manuscripts), and so do publications (IX Editions and literature). A record is known, the application's, or new, to be made (V.1 Known and new records); the registers hold the known ones before the application's first import (V.13 Registers).

Persons are common, since most files list those they name; the rest is full.

V.1 Known and new records

A record listed in a file has an xml:id, which the file's names point to by ref (#PERS.12, #new_person_Arno). The id says whether the record is known or new:

  • known: the entity exists before the file is imported, in a register or in the application's database, and the id is its own (V.13 Registers). The record holds a name for reading, and may hold nothing else; the import links the file to the entity, and never takes the record as a change;
  • new: new_, the kind's word and a name of the encoder's own (new_person_Arno, new_place_Mainz). The entity is to be made, and the record describes it, with what the guide asks of its kind. The import's dry run shows each new record with the records it may be, for whoever imports the file to match or make.

A known id is the application's: a kind's prefix and a number, a work's or a version's code (II.1.2 The coding rules), a manuscript's short code, which the application keeps beside its own id. An edition in the header names its publication by the publication's id, on the element that describes it: a volume's on its monogr, where an analytic names the text in it; the whole biblStruct's where there is no analytic (IX Editions and literature). Either is then checked as a record. A name may also point to a record the file doesn't list, by its permalink (https://…/id/person/PERS.12), which the import uses as given. An export lists every record it points to, as known records with their permalinks (X.1 Ids, permalinks and records).

V.2 Persons

A person is a person, with one main name, persName typed main, and any number typed alternative: the spellings and forms the sources use. A person's life is dated by birth, death and floruit, in the one form. Simplician of Milan, in the records of the Council of Carthage of 397, revised to the one form: born about 320, died in 400 or 401:

<person xml:id="new_person_simplicianus">
 <persName type="main">Simplician, bishop of Milan (?)</persName>
 <birth notBefore="0315" notAfter="0325"
  when-custom="0320~" datingMethod="#edtf">c. 320</birth>
 <death notBefore="0400" notAfter="0401"
  when-custom="[0400,0401]" datingMethod="#edtf">400 or 401</death>
</person>

A person's kind is its type, from a vocabulary of the application that grows, which these seed: historical, a person of the texts' times, the default, which needn't be written; modernScholar, an author or editor of a publication; divine, God, Christ, the Spirit; fictitious, a name a text takes, such as Isidorus Mercator, the name Pseudo-Isidore's preface gives its compiler. A type outside them is reported, so that a slip is seen and a new type is added on purpose. Made up:

<person xml:id="new_person_isidorus_mercator"
 type="fictitious">
 <persName type="main">Isidorus Mercator</persName>
</person>

Breaks person-term:

<person xml:id="new_person_exemplus_sanctus"
 type="saint">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 <persName type="main">Exemplus</persName>
</person>

A group of persons, the Romans, the Arians, the Novatians, is a personGrp in listPerson, with a person's id and its main name: the application keeps it as a person of the type group. A person known to belong to it joins it by a relation, memberOf, with its dates and certainty (VI.2 The types). The Novatians, whom the Council of Telepte names:

<personGrp xml:id="new_person_novatians">
 <persName type="main">Novatians</persName>
</personGrp>

A new person or group, and a register's, has one main name; a floruit gives its dates. Both are checked:

Breaks person-main-name:

<person xml:id="new_person_Secundus">
 <persName type="alternative">Secundus</persName>
</person>

Breaks floruit-dates:

<person xml:id="new_person_Quintus">
 <persName type="main">Quintus</persName>
 <floruit/>
</person>

Offices and roles, a scribe's hand, an author's works, are relations from the person (VI Relations), not parts of the record.

V.3 Places

A place is a place, with its name, placeName, and, where known, its location: the country, region and settlement it lies in, and its coordinates in geo (latitude, then longitude). Benediktbeuern, which owned a manuscript in Munich, as Mirabile gives it:

<place xml:id="new_place_benediktbeuern">
 <placeName>Benediktbeuern</placeName>
 <location>
  <country>Germania</country>
  <region>Bayern</region>
  <settlement>Benediktbeuern</settlement>
 </location>
</place>

A diocese, a province, a kingdom: each a place, its kind in type (V.9 Rare kinds and extra fields).

V.4 Institutions

An institution, a religious house, a chapter, a library, is an org, its kind in type, with one main name, orgName typed main. Where it stands is a relation to a place, as an owner's is (VI Relations). A name of an institution elsewhere in the file points to its record by ref, as other names do. The abbey of Benediktbeuern, with its id in Mirabile:

<org xml:id="new_org_benediktbeuern"
 type="religiousHouse">
 <orgName type="main">SS. Benedikt und Jakob, abbazia OSB</orgName>
 <idno type="mirabilePlace">6074</idno>
</org>
Element orgNamechange

V.5 Events

An event, a council or a synod, is an event, dated in its attributes in the one form. TEI 4.12 lets it hold a name (eventName), or a description, or paragraphs, not two of them; a name is what the import reads first. Where it took place, and what it enacted, are relations (VI Relations).

An event's type is its type, one key of the application's vocabulary of events, a narrower term its own key: council, ecumenicalCouncil, generalCouncil, provincialCouncil, synod, diocesanSynod, session, assembly, royalAssembly. The vocabulary grows, and a type outside these is reported. A new event, and a register's, has a type; a known event in another file needn't (V.1 Known and new records). The Council of Mainz of 813, as a register gives it, with a made-up id:

<event xml:id="EVENT.40"
 type="provincialCouncil" when="0813">
 <eventName>Council of Mainz, 813</eventName>
</event>

Breaks event-typed:

<event xml:id="new_event_synodus_exempli"
 when="0601">
<!-- breaks the rule: no type -->
 <eventName>Synodus exempli gratia, 601</eventName>
</event>

Breaks event-term:

<event xml:id="new_event_concilium_exempli"
 type="councel" when="0602">
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 <eventName>Concilium exempli gratia, 602</eventName>
</event>

An event points to a record by ref, or describes one, and its description has words:

Breaks event-described:

<event when="0600">
 <desc>A council.</desc>
</event>

Breaks event-words:

<event when="0601">
 <p/>
</event>
Element eventchange

V.6 Works and versions

A work, a council's canons or a collection, and each of its versions, a translation or a recension, is a bibl in listBibl, typed work or version (I.2 The levels of a text). It holds its title, its author by ref, its code (an untyped idno), its category (an idno typed category), and its date in the one form. A version is a version of its work by a relation (VI.3 The levels' relations). Mirabile's Institutiones of Justinian, from a manuscript's records, revised to the form of a work:

<listBibl>
 <bibl type="work"
  xml:id="new_work_institutiones">
  <author ref="#new_person_iustinianus">Iustinianus imperator</author>
  <title>Institutiones</title>
  <idno type="mirabileWork">2106</idno>
 </bibl>
</listBibl>

A work names its canonical version, whose canons are the work's, and, where that version has no text in the application, the version whose text stands in for it; otherwise the earliest version with a text stands in. Both are defaults the project chooses, not claims about the texts, so they are pointers in the work's record, ptr typed canonical and stand-in, to the versions' records.

A known work's canonical version has the work's own code: C.NIC-0325 is the council and its Greek canons. One file can't hold two records with one id, so the work's record stands for that version, with no record or pointer of its own, and the import makes the version from the work. Only a version with a code of its own is a record, joined to its work by versionOf (VI.3 The levels' relations). Nicaea's canons are canonical in Greek, which the corpus doesn't hold, and its first Dionysiana stands in, as the project's register of works has it:

<listBibl>
 <bibl type="work" xml:id="C.NIC-0325">
  <title>Council of Nicaea, 325</title>
  <idno>C.NIC-0325</idno>
  <idno type="category">C</idno>
  <date when="0325">325</date>
  <ptr type="stand-in"
   target="#C.NIC-0325-vDIO1"/>
 </bibl>
 <bibl type="version"
  xml:id="C.NIC-0325-vDIO1">
  <title>Council of Nicaea, 325, in the first Dionysiana</title>
  <idno>C.NIC-0325-vDIO1</idno>
 </bibl>
</listBibl>
<listRelation>
 <relation name="versionOf"
  active="#C.NIC-0325-vDIO1" passive="#C.NIC-0325"/>
</listRelation>

A new work, not yet in the application, names its versions' records by their new ids, its canonical version among them. A made-up council, canonical in Greek, its Latin version standing in:

<listBibl>
 <bibl type="work"
  xml:id="new_work_concilium_exempli">
  <title>Council of Exemplum</title>
  <ptr type="canonical"
   target="#new_version_concilium_exempli_graece"/>
  <ptr type="stand-in"
   target="#new_version_concilium_exempli_latine"/>
 </bibl>
 <bibl type="version"
  xml:id="new_version_concilium_exempli_graece">
  <title>Council of Exemplum, the Greek canons</title>
 </bibl>
 <bibl type="version"
  xml:id="new_version_concilium_exempli_latine">
  <title>Council of Exemplum, in a Latin translation</title>
 </bibl>
</listBibl>

A work cited by its siglum in Gryson's Répertoire holds the siglum in its record, an idno typed gryson, whether its code is made from the siglum (W.IS-ety) or is its own, as a pope's letter's is: a pointer by the siglum reaches its file through it (IV.4 Sources and the pointers' forms). The file doesn't hold the siglum, since its formal title holds only its code, category, type and levels. Gregory's letter IX, 219, in the register of works:

<listBibl>
 <bibl type="work" xml:id="D.GRE-0599-1748">
  <title>Gregory I to Aregio episcopo Franciis, 599 (Register IX, 219)</title>
  <idno>D.GRE-0599-1748</idno>
  <idno type="category">D</idno>
  <idno type="gryson">GR-M ep 9, 219</idno>
 </bibl>
</listBibl>

Breaks version-choices:

   target="#new_version_exemplum_latin"/>
  <ptr type="canonical"
   target="#new_version_exemplum_greek"/>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 </bibl>
</listBibl>

V.7 Witnesses

A witness is a copy of a text: in a manuscript, at its folios, or in a publication, at its pages (I.2 The levels of a text). A file names the witnesses it needs in the source description's listWit, each a witness with an xml:id, holding a pointer to its manuscript or publication and its folios or pages: an edition carrying the page breaks of several manuscripts points to each from its page breaks by edRef (III.10.2 Page breaks), and a reconstruction lists the witnesses its editor reconstructed it from (IX Editions and literature). A made-up list:

<listWit>
 <witness xml:id="wit-M">
  <bibl>
   <ptr target="#new_manuscript_munich_4592"/>
  </bibl>, <locus from="158r" to="207v">ff. 158r–207v</locus>
 </witness>
 <witness xml:id="wit-E">
  <bibl>
   <ptr target="#new_bibl_maassen_1870"/>
   <citedRange unit="page" from="852"
    to="853">852–853</citedRange>
  </bibl>
 </witness>
</listWit>

The import joins each witness to its manuscript or publication, at its folios or pages.

V.8 Work groups

A work group is a scholarly construct over several works, "the Pseudo-Isidorian forgeries": a bibl typed workGroup in listBibl, as works and versions are, with its name in title, what it is in a note, and its type in subtype, the key of one of the application's types of group: oneOrigin, works of one origin; derivedCollections, a collection and those derived from it; oneManuscript, the collections of one manuscript; projectGrouping, the project's own grouping. Another key is reported, since the application's vocabulary grows, as an event's type is. Its members join it by relations, memberOf, each from the member, a work, a version or another group, with its certainty and evidence, since membership is a claim (VI.2 The types): the Clavis canonum counts the Collectio Lanfranci among the Pseudo-Isidorian forgeries, an abridgement made two centuries later. A made-up group, its ids made up too:

<listBibl>
 <bibl type="workGroup" subtype="oneOrigin"
  xml:id="GROUP.2">
  <title>The Pseudo-Isidorian forgeries</title>
  <note>The forgeries made in the circle of the False Decretals, in the middle of the 9th century.</note>
 </bibl>
</listBibl>
<listRelation>
 <relation name="memberOf" type="judgement"
  active="COLL.PsIsidore" passive="#GROUP.2"/>
 <relation name="memberOf" type="judgement"
  cert="low" active="COLL.Lanfranc" passive="#GROUP.2">
  <desc>after <bibl>
    <ptr target="#PUBL.5"/>
   </bibl>
  </desc>
 </relation>
</listRelation>

A group made as a part of a larger one, by whoever made the larger, points to it by corresp, since containment is a pointer; a group that someone else counts in a larger construct is a member of it, like any other. A group whose members stand in an order, Gregory I's Register by book and letter, a ruler's capitularies by date, gives each member's place in n on its relation: a whole number, counting from 1 in each group. The order is optional: a group in no order, as the Pseudo-Isidorian forgeries, has none, and neither has a member whose place isn't known. A made-up group, a register that is a part of it with two letters in their order, and a letter outside the register, a member of the larger group with no place:

<listBibl>
 <bibl type="workGroup" subtype="oneOrigin"
  xml:id="new_group_letters_exempli">
  <title>The letters of Exemplus</title>
 </bibl>
 <bibl type="workGroup" subtype="oneOrigin"
  xml:id="new_group_register_exempli" corresp="#new_group_letters_exempli">
  <title>The register of Exemplus</title>
  <note>The letters his chancery copied into its register, in the register's order.</note>
 </bibl>
</listBibl>
<listRelation>
 <relation name="memberOf" type="judgement"
  active="D.EXE-0601-12" passive="#new_group_register_exempli"
  n="1"/>
 <relation name="memberOf" type="judgement"
  active="D.EXE-0601-15" passive="#new_group_register_exempli"
  n="2"/>
 <relation name="memberOf" type="judgement"
  active="D.EXE-0602-3" passive="#new_group_letters_exempli"/>
</listRelation>

A place is a whole number from 1, and two members of one group don't share one in a file; both are reported:

Breaks member-place-number:

<listRelation>
 <relation name="memberOf" type="judgement"
  active="D.EXE-0601-12" passive="#new_group_register_exempli"
  n="first"/>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
</listRelation>

Breaks member-place-unique:

<listRelation>
 <relation name="memberOf" type="judgement"
  active="D.EXE-0601-12" passive="#new_group_register_exempli"

V.9 Rare kinds and extra fields

A record of a kind the guide has no section for, a diocese or a religious order, takes the nearest form, with its kind in type: a diocese is a place, an order an org. A name of it in the text is an rs, its kind in type and its record in ref (IV.8 What no element fits). The kinds and their fields are defined in the application, as in Heurist; a field the form has no element for is an f, named as the field, in an fs typed by the kind, in a note typed extra: TEI lets a place, an institution or an event hold an fs only in a note, and every kind does the same. A made-up diocese:

<place xml:id="new_place_diocese_exemplum"
 type="diocese">
 <placeName>Dioecesis Exemplensis</placeName>
 <note type="extra">
  <fs type="diocese">
   <f name="metropolitan">Provincia Exemplensis</f>
  </fs>
 </note>
</place>

Only a kind that no form fits needs a new section of the guide.

V.10 Ids elsewhere

A record's ids in other projects' catalogues are idnos, each typed by its authority, its key in lower camel case. The guide knows wikidata, viaf, gnd, geonames, tgn, pleiades, Mirabile's five indices (below), clavis, biblissima, schoenberg, hmml, bodleian, manusOnline, the Bodleian's ieArk, crArk and SCN, a library's ISIL (isil), a publication's doi and its records in k10plus, the dnb and openAlex, Gryson's sigla (gryson), Kéry's catalogue (kery), the World Historical Gazetteer (whg), and Jaffé's register of the popes' letters (jaffe). The authorities grow: another is reported, unless the file declares it, with its links (II.4.3 Other projects' ids). The import keeps the ids beside the record's own, and matches by them. Justinian, as Mirabile names him, with his id there:

<person xml:id="new_person_iustinianus">
 <persName type="main">Iustinianus imperator</persName>
 <persName type="alternative">Flavius Petrus Sabbatius Iustinianus</persName>
 <persName type="alternative">Justinian I</persName>
 <idno type="mirabileAuthor">19360</idno>
</person>

Mirabile numbers each of its indices on its own: 20174 is a work in its index of titles, and another record in its index of authors. A person may be in two of them, its authors and the people its manuscripts name. So each index is an authority of its own, with its key and one link, the index's address followed by the number; the import makes the link, and an export writes the number:

Mirabile's indices, each an authority

A single key mirabile is reported, as any key the guide doesn't list; the id's key follows from its record's kind: a person's mirabileAuthor, a work's or a version's mirabileWork, an institution's mirabilePlace, a manuscript's mirabileManuscript.

Links to pages that aren't an authority's record, a library's page on a person or a scholar's on a work, go in a note typed links, holding a list of links, each with its target and words:

<person xml:id="new_person_exemplus_linked">
 <persName type="main">Exemplus</persName>
 <note type="links">
  <list type="links">
   <item>
    <ref target="https://example.org/exemplus">A page on Exemplus</ref>
   </item>
  </list>
 </note>
</person>

An authority's record linked there is reported, since its id goes in an idno; the import reads it as the id, as it reads the earlier files' notes typed authorities, which hold an authority's numbers. A note of links holds only its list, and each link its target and words:

Breaks link-authority:

<person xml:id="new_person_Sextus">
 <persName type="main">Sextus</persName>
 <note type="links">

V.11 Sources

Every record, text and annotation names where it comes from. A record's source is a bibl typed source in it: the source's name, the record's id there (an idno typed by the authority), a link to it (ref), the date it was taken (date typed retrieved), and the licence it was taken under (licence, in availability). The import keeps it as the record's provenance. The Institutiones again, with its source:

<listBibl>
 <bibl type="work"
  xml:id="new_work_institutiones_sourced">
  <title>Institutiones</title>
  <bibl type="source">
   <title>Mirabile</title>
   <idno type="mirabileWork">2106</idno>
   <ref target="https://www.mirabileweb.it/title/-title/2106">mirabileweb.it</ref>
   <date type="retrieved" when="2025-01-10">10 January 2025</date>
  </bibl>
 </bibl>
</listBibl>

The date retrieved is made up, since the file doesn't give it; Mirabile states no licence for its records, and so none is given. A made-up place taken from Wikidata, whose data is under CC0:

 <placeName>Exemplum</placeName>
 <bibl type="source">
  <title>Wikidata</title>
  <date type="retrieved" when="2026-11-04">4 November 2026</date>
  <availability>
   <licence target="https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/">CC0 1.0</licence>

A text taken from another project names its source in the same form, in its header (II.3 Publication and source).

V.12 Literature

A record's literature is the publications that discuss it or cite it, as a record's source is where its data was taken from. Each is a bibl in the record, without a type: a ptr to the publication's record and its pages in citedRange, as a witness and a relation's evidence point to a publication, and in its words how the publication cites the record. Several publications are several bibls. A made-up bishop, and the page that names him:

<person xml:id="new_person_exemplus_literature">
 <persName type="main">Exemplus, bishop of Exemplum</persName>
 <bibl>
  <ptr target="#PUBL.5"/>
  <citedRange unit="page" from="112"
   to="114">112–114</citedRange>, as "Exemplus
   episcopus", among a council's signatories</bibl>
</person>

Literature is never a relation: no type of the guide's says it (VI.2 The types), and a relation named referencedIn, the application's own name for it, is reported as any type outside the list is. The import keeps each publication with its pages and words; an export writes a record's literature in the same form.

V.13 Registers

A register is a file of records of one kind, the seed of the application's database. The data project makes one for each kind, persons with their groups, places, institutions with the libraries, events, works with their versions and the work groups, manuscripts, publications, from authoritative lists (the popes, the emperors, the councils, the Clavis canonum's libraries, Gryson's works) and from every set's placeholders, reconciled and reviewed. The application loads the registers first, then the files, which name the registers' records by their ids. After that the database is the records' truth, and the registers' form is how it exports its lists.

A register is a header and a standOff, without a text. Its formal title holds its type, register, alone: a register has no code and no category, since it isn't a document (II.1.1 Categories and types). Its source description names every list it's made from, each a bibl typed source, and its publication statement its licence. Its records stand in the lists of their kind, each in the guide's form, its xml:id its known id, with its sources, its ids elsewhere, and its relations in a listRelation: a group's members, a work group's members, a version's work, with their certainty and evidence. A work's record stands for its canonical version, which has the work's code; only versions with codes of their own are records (V.6 Works and versions). Records point to each other by their ids (#INST.3 for a manuscript's library, #PERS.88 for a publication's editor), which may stand in another register: the registers resolve as a set, which the data project checks and the import reports on. A register's records are held to what the guide asks of a new record (V.1 Known and new records).

A record not reviewed yet says so in a note typed unreviewed, its words saying what wasn't checked; the application reads it as the record's trust mark, "not checked". A register of persons, its records and sources made up:
