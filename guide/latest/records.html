The SOLEMNE Encoding Guidelines

V Records

A record is a thing the texts are about, or come from, which the application keeps once and every file points to: a person, a place, an institution, an event, a manuscript, a publication, a work and its versions. A file names records where its text names them (IV.1 Names and their records), and may list them, with what it knows of them, in its standOff: persons in listPerson, places in listPlace, institutions in listOrg, events in listEvent, works, versions and publications in listBibl. Every record may have its names, its dates in the one form (VII Dates), its ids elsewhere, its sources, notes, and its relations to other records (VI Relations). Manuscripts have a part of their own (VIII Manuscripts), and publications will (IX Editions and literature).

Persons are common, since most files list those they name; the rest is full.

V.1 Placeholders, ids and permalinks

A record listed in a file has an xml:id, which the file's names point to by ref (#new_person_Arno):

  • a placeholder's, for a record the application doesn't have yet: new_, the kind, and a name of the encoder's own (new_person_Arno, new_place_Mainz); the import makes a provisional record of it, with what the entry holds, for the project to identify;
  • a record's, for one it has: its kind and its number (PERS.1234); a work's or a version's is its code (C.NIC-0325).

A name may also point to a record the file doesn't list, by its permalink (https://…/id/person/PERS.1234), which the import uses as given. An export points by permalinks, and lists the records it points to, with their names, so that the file reads on its own (X Exports). The records of the Council of Carthage of 397 list its two councils by placeholders, with their dates:

<listEvent>
 <event xml:id="new_event_carthage_0397"
  when="0397">
  <eventName>Council of Carthage, 397</eventName>
 </event>
 <event xml:id="new_event_hippo_0393"
  when="0393">
  <eventName>Council of Hippo, 393</eventName>
 </event>
</listEvent>

V.2 Persons

A person is a person, with one main name, persName typed main, and any number typed alternative: the spellings and forms the sources use. A person's life is dated by birth, death and floruit, in the one form. Simplician of Milan, in the records of the Council of Carthage of 397, revised to the one form: born about 320, died in 400 or 401:

<person xml:id="new_person_simplicianus">
 <persName type="main">Simplician, bishop of Milan (?)</persName>
 <birth notBefore="0315" notAfter="0325"
  when-custom="0320~" datingMethod="#edtf">c. 320</birth>
 <death notBefore="0400" notAfter="0401"
  when-custom="[0400,0401]" datingMethod="#edtf">400 or 401</death>
</person>

A person in the standOff has a record's id or a placeholder's; a placeholder's person has one main name; a floruit gives its dates. Each is checked:

Breaks person-id:

<person xml:id="person_7">
 <persName type="main">Exemplus</persName>
</person>

Breaks person-main-name:

<person xml:id="new_person_Secundus">
 <persName type="alternative">Secundus</persName>
</person>

Breaks floruit-dates:

<person xml:id="new_person_Quintus">
 <persName type="main">Quintus</persName>
 <floruit/>
</person>

Offices and roles, a scribe's hand, an author's works, are relations from the person (VI Relations), not parts of the record.

Element personchange
Element floruitchange

V.3 Places

A place is a place, with its name, placeName, and, where known, its location: the country, region and settlement it lies in, and its coordinates in geo (latitude, then longitude). Benediktbeuern, which owned a manuscript in Munich, as Mirabile gives it:

<place xml:id="new_place_benediktbeuern">
 <placeName>Benediktbeuern</placeName>
 <location>
  <country>Germania</country>
  <region>Bayern</region>
  <settlement>Benediktbeuern</settlement>
 </location>
</place>

A diocese, a province, a kingdom: each a place, its kind in type (V.9 Rare kinds and extra fields).

V.4 Institutions

An institution, a religious house, a chapter, a library, is an org, its kind in type, with one main name, orgName typed main. Where it stands is a relation to a place, as an owner's is (VI Relations). A name of an institution elsewhere in the file points to its record by ref, as other names do. The abbey of Benediktbeuern, with its id in Mirabile:

<org xml:id="new_org_benediktbeuern"
 type="religiousHouse">
 <orgName type="main">SS. Benedikt und Jakob, abbazia OSB</orgName>
 <idno type="mirabile">6074</idno>
</org>
Element orgNamechange

V.5 Events

An event, a council or a synod, is an event, dated in its attributes in the one form. TEI 4.12 lets it hold a name (eventName), or a description, or paragraphs, not two of them; a name is what the import reads first. Where it took place, and what it enacted, are relations (VI Relations). An event points to a record by ref, or describes one, and its description has words:

Breaks event-described:

<event when="0600">
 <desc>A council.</desc>
</event>

Breaks event-words:

<event when="0601">
 <p/>
</event>
Element eventchange

V.6 Works and versions

A work, a council's canons or a collection, and each of its versions, a translation or a recension, is a bibl in listBibl, typed work or version (I.2 The levels of a text). It holds its title, its author by ref, its code (an untyped idno), its category (an idno typed category), and its date in the one form. A version is a version of its work by a relation (VI.3 The levels' relations). Mirabile's Institutiones of Justinian, from a manuscript's records, revised to the form of a work:

<listBibl>
 <bibl type="work"
  xml:id="new_work_institutiones">
  <author ref="#new_person_iustinianus">Iustinianus imperator</author>
  <title>Institutiones</title>
  <idno type="mirabile">2106</idno>
 </bibl>
</listBibl>

A work names its canonical version, whose canons are the work's, and, where that version has no text in the application, the version whose text stands in for it; otherwise the earliest version with a text stands in. Both are defaults the project chooses, not claims about the texts, so they are pointers in the work's record, ptr typed canonical and stand-in, to the versions' records. Nicaea's canons are canonical in Greek, which the corpus doesn't hold; a made-up record lets a Latin version stand in:

<listBibl>
 <bibl type="work" xml:id="C.NIC-0325">
  <title>Council of Nicaea, 325</title>
  <idno>C.NIC-0325</idno>
  <idno type="category">C</idno>
  <date when="0325">325</date>
  <ptr type="canonical"
   target="#new_version_nicaea_greek"/>
  <ptr type="stand-in"
   target="#new_version_nicaea_dionysius"/>
 </bibl>
 <bibl type="version"
  xml:id="new_version_nicaea_greek">
  <title>Council of Nicaea, 325, the Greek canons</title>
 </bibl>
 <bibl type="version"
  xml:id="new_version_nicaea_dionysius">
  <title>Council of Nicaea, 325, in Dionysius Exiguus's translation</title>
 </bibl>
</listBibl>

A work names at most one canonical version and one stand-in, and a pointer of another type is an error:

Breaks version-choices:

<listBibl>
 <bibl type="work"
  xml:id="new_work_exemplum_bis">
  <title>Exemplum</title>
  <ptr type="canonical"
   target="#new_version_exemplum_latin"/>
  <ptr type="canonical"
   target="#new_version_exemplum_greek"/>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 </bibl>
</listBibl>

Breaks pointer-type:

<listBibl>
 <bibl type="work"
  xml:id="new_work_exemplum">
  <title>Exemplum</title>
  <ptr type="best"
   target="#new_version_exemplum"/>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
 </bibl>
</listBibl>

The import makes a provisional work or version of a placeholder, and reads its canonical and stand-in choices; an export writes each work and version it points to, with them.

Element ptrchange

V.7 Witnesses

A witness is a copy of a text: in a manuscript, at its folios, or in a publication, at its pages (I.2 The levels of a text). A file names the witnesses it needs in the source description's listWit, each a witness with an xml:id, holding a pointer to its manuscript or publication and its folios or pages: an edition carrying the page breaks of several manuscripts points to each from its page breaks by edRef (III.9.2 Page breaks), and a reconstruction lists the witnesses its editor reconstructed it from (IX Editions and literature). A made-up list:

<listWit>
 <witness xml:id="wit-M">
  <bibl>
   <ptr target="#new_manuscript_munich_4592"/>
  </bibl>, <locus from="158r" to="207v">ff. 158r–207v</locus>
 </witness>
 <witness xml:id="wit-E">
  <bibl>
   <ptr target="#new_bibl_maassen_1870"/>
   <citedRange unit="page" from="852"
    to="853">852–853</citedRange>
  </bibl>
 </witness>
</listWit>

The import joins each witness to its manuscript or publication, at its folios or pages.

V.8 Work groups

A work group is a scholarly construct over several works, "the Pseudo-Isidorian forgeries": a listBibl typed workGroup, with its name in head, what it is in desc, and its members, each a bibl holding a ptr to the work's record (TEI lets a list of bibliography hold only bibliographic items). Every heading is typed (III.2 Headings); a group's name is typed rubric, which says nothing more there. A made-up group:

<listBibl type="workGroup"
 xml:id="new_group_pseudo_isidore">
 <head type="rubric">The Pseudo-Isidorian forgeries</head>
 <desc>The forgeries made in the circle of the False Decretals, in the middle of the 9th century.</desc>
 <bibl>
  <ptr target="#new_work_false_decretals"/>
 </bibl>
 <bibl>
  <ptr target="#new_work_benedictus_levita"/>
 </bibl>
</listBibl>

A group is stored with its name, description, author and members; it is never a level of a text.

V.9 Rare kinds and extra fields

A record of a kind the guide has no section for, a diocese or a religious order, takes the nearest form, with its kind in type: a diocese is a place, an order an org. A name of it in the text is an rs, its kind in type and its record in ref (IV.8 What no element fits). The kinds and their fields are defined in the application, as in Heurist; a field the form has no element for is an f, named as the field, in an fs typed by the kind, in a note typed extra: TEI lets a place, an institution or an event hold an fs only in a note, and every kind does the same. A made-up diocese:

<place xml:id="new_place_diocese_exemplum"
 type="diocese">
 <placeName>Dioecesis Exemplensis</placeName>
 <note type="extra">
  <fs type="diocese">
   <f name="metropolitan">Provincia Exemplensis</f>
  </fs>
 </note>
</place>

Only a kind that no form fits needs a new section of the guide.

V.10 Ids elsewhere

A record's ids in other projects' catalogues are idnos, each typed by its authority, from a closed list: wikidata, viaf, gnd, geonames, tgn, pleiades, mirabile, clavis, biblissima, schoenberg, hmml, bodleian, manusOnline, and the Bodleian's ieArk, crArk and SCN (II.4.3 Other projects' ids). The import keeps them beside the record's own id, and matches by them. Justinian, as Mirabile names him, with his id there:

<person xml:id="new_person_iustinianus">
 <persName type="main">Iustinianus imperator</persName>
 <persName type="alternative">Flavius Petrus Sabbatius Iustinianus</persName>
 <persName type="alternative">Justinian I</persName>
 <idno type="mirabile">19360</idno>
</person>

An authority may number each kind of record on its own. Mirabile does: 20174 is a work in its index of titles, and another record in its index of authors. The kind of record says which index its number is from, and the import makes the link from it, the index's address followed by a name and the number, as the corpus's links have them; an export writes the number:

Mirabile's indices, by the kind of record
RecordIndexLink
a personauthorshttps://www.mirabileweb.it/author/-author/19840
a work or a versiontitleshttps://www.mirabileweb.it/title/-/20174
a manuscriptmanuscriptshttps://www.mirabileweb.it/manuscript/-manuscript/220110

Mirabile's other indices, such as its institutions', join the table as the application reads them. Bernardus of Pavia and his Summa decretalium, each with the number of its own index:

<person xml:id="new_person_bernardus_papiensis">
 <persName type="main">Bernardus Papiensis praepositus</persName>
 <idno type="mirabile">19840</idno>
</person>
<listBibl>
 <bibl type="work"
  xml:id="new_work_summa_decretalium">
  <title>Summa decretalium</title>
  <author ref="#new_person_bernardus_papiensis">Bernardus Papiensis</author>
  <idno type="mirabile">20174</idno>
 </bibl>
</listBibl>

Links to pages that aren't an authority's record, a library's page on a person or a scholar's on a work, go in a note typed links, holding a list of links, each with its target and words:

<person xml:id="new_person_exemplus_linked">
 <persName type="main">Exemplus</persName>
 <note type="links">
  <list type="links">
   <item>
    <ref target="https://example.org/exemplus">A page on Exemplus</ref>
   </item>
  </list>
 </note>
</person>

An authority's record linked there is reported, since its id goes in an idno; the import reads it as the id, as it reads the earlier files' notes typed authorities, which hold an authority's numbers. A note of links holds only its list, and each link its target and words:

Breaks link-authority:

<person xml:id="new_person_Sextus">
 <persName type="main">Sextus</persName>
 <note type="links">
  <list type="links">
   <item>
    <ref target="https://www.mirabileweb.it/author/-author/19840">Mirabile</ref>
   </item>
<!-- breaks the rule -->
  </list>
 </note>
</person>

Breaks links-list:

<person xml:id="new_person_Tertius">
 <persName type="main">Tertius</persName>
 <note type="links">See the catalogue.</note>
</person>

Breaks link-target:

<person xml:id="new_person_Quartus">
 <persName type="main">Quartus</persName>
 <note type="links">
  <list type="links">
   <item>
    <ref target="https://example.org/person/7"/>
   </item>
  </list>
 </note>
</person>
Element listchange

V.11 Sources

Every record, text and annotation names where it comes from. A record's source is a bibl typed source in it: the source's name, the record's id there (an idno typed by the authority), a link to it (ref), the date it was taken (date typed retrieved), and the licence it was taken under (licence, in availability). The import keeps it as the record's provenance. The Institutiones again, with its source:

<listBibl>
 <bibl type="work"
  xml:id="new_work_institutiones_sourced">
  <title>Institutiones</title>
  <bibl type="source">
   <title>Mirabile</title>
   <idno type="mirabile">2106</idno>
   <ref target="http://www.mirabileweb.it/title/-/2106">mirabileweb.it</ref>
   <date type="retrieved" when="2025-01-10">10 January 2025</date>
  </bibl>
 </bibl>
</listBibl>

The date retrieved is made up, since the file doesn't give it; Mirabile states no licence for its records, and so none is given. A made-up place taken from Wikidata, whose data is under CC0:

<place xml:id="new_place_exemplum_sourced">
 <placeName>Exemplum</placeName>
 <bibl type="source">
  <title>Wikidata</title>
  <date type="retrieved" when="2026-11-04">4 November 2026</date>
  <availability>
   <licence target="https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/">CC0 1.0</licence>
  </availability>
 </bibl>
</place>

A text taken from another project names its source in the same form, in its header (II.3 Publication and source).
